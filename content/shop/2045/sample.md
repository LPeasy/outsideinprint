Morning light filtered through the apartment’s automatic blinds, right on time. An Optimus hummed as it blended his favorite smoothie. He didn’t like smoothies anymore. “Good morning,” the bot chirped, handing him the drink. Its fingers were delicate and nimble. The synthetic voice grated on him—too cheerful, too perfect. He stared at the smoothie for a moment before setting it on the counter. He wouldn’t drink it. Across the room, another bot neatly folded his laundry. It pressed each shirt with mechanical precision before stacking them in a perfect tower. “Is there anything else I can optimize for you today?” the Optimus asked. He waved it off, sinking into the couch. The apartment was spotless, the air subtly perfumed with lavender—a scent calibrated to reduce stress. He remembered how his mother used to leave the windows open, letting in the smell of cut grass and the sound of distant lawnmowers. Nothing perfumed. Nothing optimized. The purifier ran on.

At lunch, Jenna waved at him from their table at the cafe. Lucas and Tom were sitting there chomping at the bit and chopping it up like professional chefs. “Zero Sum says humanity’s won 4,000 consecutive victories this week,” Tom quipped. “Hard not to win when the scoreboard’s just a looped gif of fireworks,” Lucas snickered.

Jenna rolled her eyes. She turned to him as he sat down. “You should come to the studio,” she beamed. “Pottery’s messy. That’s half the point.”

“Know why I started doing this? My grandmother used to make these ugly little clay frogs when she was stressed.” She crinkled her nose. “They were lumpy and ridiculous, but they made her happy. She didn’t care if they weren’t perfect—she just loved making them!”

Then she looked right at him and said, “You should come Thursday. Get clay under your nails.”

His mouth twitched. “And what am I supposed to do? Pour my existential dread into a vase?”

Jenna’s eyes narrowed. “Why not? At least then it’d be worth looking at.”

“Sounds pointless,” he muttered, looking at his plate, poking at his salad. “You make pots, then what? Let them sit on a shelf?”

Jenna sighed. She picked up a piece of bread, tore it in half, and left the pieces on her plate. “Maybe try making one ugly thing on purpose,” she said. She poked him in the chest.

“You’d hate it,” she said. “Which is why you should come.”

He left lunch early, pretending to have a meeting.

That night, he watched his Optimus fold towels with machine precision. Suddenly, it froze mid-motion, one arm outstretched like a damn Nazi. Its head twitched violently, it beeped three times, and then it said: “Optimizing… optimizing…”

The room held still except for the hum of its internal mechanisms.

Then all hell broke loose.

Across the hallway, he heard a neighbor scream, “My soup!” followed by the unmistakable crash of shattering porcelain. Outside, delivery drones wobbled in the air, dropping packages like they were carpet-bombing the neighborhood.

The news anchor’s voice cut through the chaos, overly calm: “The Optimus optimization fault is being addressed and is no cause for alarm. Please enjoy this complimentary meditation playlist brought to you by Artisify.”

Meanwhile, his own bot began spinning like an unbalanced ceiling fan, flinging perfectly folded towels into the air. One landed on his head, draping him like a disheveled ghost. He blinked beneath the fabric, then burst out laughing. For the first time in years, he folded his own towels. It wasn’t perfect—one corner stubbornly refused to align, and he had to refold half of them.

Jenna’s clay frogs flashed through his mind. The next day, he sent her a message: *Still have room in that pottery class?*

The clay resisted him. It stuck to his fingers, collapsed under his touch, and refused to be shaped. Around him, others laughed at their failures, embracing the imperfection of their creations, but he felt only frustration. “This is pointless,” he threw the lump of clay down with a wet thud. His elbow bumped a finished pot on the table behind him, sending it wobbling dangerously before a classmate caught it. He muttered an apology without meeting anyone’s eyes.

Jenna, sitting across from him, wiped her hands on her apron. “Pointless?” she echoed. “Look around you.” She gestured to the room, where laughter echoed as classmates admired each other’s crooked vases and lopsided bowls. He folded his arms and glanced at the chaos Jenna seemed so at peace with. “Great. So, I’m supposed to fix my life with\.\.\. misshapen pottery?” Jenna leaned forward. “Then make a misshapen pot,” she said. “Make ten. Clay is cheap.” He didn’t respond. Without another word, he stood, brushing the clay from his hands, and left the studio. He never came back.

The next few days blurred together. The bots were fixed. Ads flashed on his screen: “Feeling stuck? Unlock your potential with Artisify! Create. Connect. Struggle™.” He scoffed. He tried cooking. The recipe app chirped instructions in a saccharine tone while he charred pasta into an inedible lump and accidentally ignited a paper towel. Smoke filled the kitchen, triggering the automatic sprinklers, which sprayed him and the Optimus indiscriminately.

He tried doing laundry. Things were going well until the washing machine jammed mid-spin cycle. Clothes spewed onto the floor and sudsy bubbles poured into his apartment. He laughed once before he could stop himself. Then the wet socks and soap on the floor ruined it.

Jenna stopped responding to his messages. Lucas sent a curt text: *“You overthink everything.”* Tom made a joke about him becoming a robot, and it stung more than he knew. He wasn’t sure if they were still his friends.

One evening, his Optimus froze again, mid-laundry. “Optimizing… optimizing…” it repeated, its voice teetering on the edge of politeness. Its unmoving hand still gripped a towel like a peace offering. Something inside him snapped. He grabbed a chair and swung wildly, smashing the bot’s glossy shell. Pieces of plastic and circuitry scattered across the room like confetti for a celebration no one wanted. He stood over the wreckage. For a second, it wasn’t a bot he saw wrecked on the ground—it was a memory, some half-forgotten funeral, the smell of lilies and burnt coffee. The towel lay at his feet, immaculately clean and perfectly folded.

Jenna visited a week later. He gestured to the shattered bot in the corner like a child presenting a broken toy. “Well,” he said, forcing a hollow laugh, “I guess I finally made a mess worth looking at.” She didn’t laugh. She tilted her head, examining the wreckage with pity and frustration. “Is this what you wanted? Some big problem you could point at?” She looked back at him. “Well, congratulations. You’ve got it.” She reached into her bag and pulled out one of her pots, cracked and imperfect but lovingly glazed. Setting it on the table, she ran her fingers along its ridges, her touch deliberate. “I almost threw this one away,” she said quietly. “It didn’t come out right. But now I think it’s my favorite. It sits by my window now. I keep my keys in it.” Without another word, she turned and walked out, leaving the pot on the table.

He stared at the pot, its uneven surface catching the dim light. The cracks seemed deeper now, like fault lines. He leaned back in his chair. Just then, an Optimus glided past his window, its polished face catching the dusk. The bot carried a serene expression, and a subtle badge on its chest displayed a new feature: “Artificial Empathy™.” It spoke.

“Don’t worry, friend,” it said, warm and even. “I’m here to help you get unstuck.” With a quiet hum, it extended a perfectly wrapped gift box labeled “Purpose Kit.” Beneath the bow, the phrase “Your journey begins here” shone in soft blue letters.

He stared at the bot, then at the pot, its cracks catching the last rays of the sun. His hand lingered on the uneven surface. He chuckled, a dry, hollow sound. The Optimus waited, its outstretched hand unwavering, the “Purpose Kit” steady in the dim light.

“Artificial Empathy,” he muttered, shaking his head. “A robot that cares. How\.\.\. optimal.” He looked at the bot, its serene expression unchanging. His eyes darted to the “Purpose Kit,” its promise waiting in the dim light. For a moment, his fingers loosened on the pot. What if the kit really would help?

The Optimus stood motionless. He hesitated, his fingers tight around the pot while his gaze lingered on the box.
