Oliver pressed the corner of his name tag against his jacket. It stayed down until he took his hand away.

Syd was still writing his name.

**Oliver:** There's a space for your company.

**Syd:** I saw.

He put the pen back in the cup.

Through the hotel bar's open doors, somebody laughed over the end of an introduction. A woman at the registration table handed Oliver two drink tokens and pointed towards the coat rack.

He gave one token to Syd.

**Syd:** What did you put?

**Oliver:** My name.

**Syd:** Under company.

**Oliver:** Marketing.

**Syd:** All of it?

Oliver took out his phone.

**Oliver:** Yeah. I updated my LinkedIn profile again.

**Syd:** Again?

He turned the screen. In the picture, Oliver sat behind an open laptop. Across its lid were the words: I AM A VERY SERIOUS MARKETING PROFESSIONAL WHO TAKES MYSELF VERY SERIOUSLY.

Syd leaned closer.

**Syd:** Is... that an actual sticker on your laptop?

**Oliver:** I put it on the picture.

**Syd:** Remember when people used to call that a “photoshop”?

**Oliver:** It's a character. A marketing director who thinks every little thing he does is changing the world.

**Syd:** Have you posted anything?

Oliver scrolled.

**Oliver:** Three. This one's done quite well.

Syd read it aloud.

**Syd:** I have replaced our employee suggestion box with a mirror. Ownership starts with you.

He laughed.

**Oliver:** There's going to be videos too. It's gonna be skits.

**Syd:** Same man?

**Oliver:** Yes. I haven't actually tried to, like, manifest the character yet.

**Syd:** He's got your face.

**Oliver:** Pretty good face, isn't it?

They joined the queue at the bar. Oliver slid his phone into his pocket. His name tag lifted again.

**Syd:** What do people say?

**Oliver:** Mostly they laugh. One man wrote a long comment about how managers have to make difficult decisions.

**Syd:** Did you answer?

**Oliver:** I thanked him for his leadership, and saluted with an emoji.

**Syd:** Perfect.

**Oliver:** He liked it.

The man in front of them moved aside with two glasses. Before Oliver could order, a woman touched his arm.

“You're the very serious marketing professional.” She leaned towards him. “I've been hearing about you all evening,” she whispered in his ear.

Oliver smiled.

“I liked the suggestion box,” she said, laughing. “Nora. We spoke at the breakfast in June.”

“Yes,” Oliver said. “How are you?”

“Still doing the swimming. Actually, could I ask you about that? We're trying to fill the adult beginners' classes.”

The bartender looked at Oliver. He ordered two beers. Syd handed over his token, and Oliver added the other.

Nora took a card from her bag.

“The page on the website is terrible. People keep ringing to ask whether it's for their children. Do you do that sort of thing?”

“Yes,” Oliver said.

“Oh, good. Could I send it to you?”

“Of course.”

Someone called her from a table near the windows. She put the card in Oliver's hand.

“I'll find you before I go.”

The bartender set down their beers. Syd took his and followed Oliver to a narrow table against the wall.

**Syd:** How about that! It worked. I almost can't believe it.

**Oliver:** She doesn't know it's a joke.

**Syd:** Wait, she laughed... didn't she?

Oliver took a drink. Across the room, Nora was helping a man move his chair out of the path to the bar.

Oliver turned the card over. There was a timetable on the back.

At the next table, a man was explaining why he stopped calling his employees “employees.” Syd looked at Oliver.

**Oliver:** What?

**Syd:** I didn't say anything.

**Oliver:** Shh! I'm trying to hear what he calls them.

They listened.

**Syd:** Partners.

**Oliver:** I have associates.

Syd drank his beer. Oliver pressed his name tag down once more, then peeled it off and laid it beside his glass.

**Syd:** Is that the type of man who the character is for?

**Oliver:** Partly. And for the people who have to sit through meetings with guys like him all day.

**Syd:** He might think it's for him too.

**Oliver:** I'm not going to explain every joke, Syd. That defeats the purpose.

**Syd:** I wouldn't ask you to.

Nora came over carrying her coat.

“I've got to get back. Is that your email on the profile?”

“It is,” Oliver said. “Send me the page. I'll have a look and tell you what it needs. Then we can talk business.”

“Lovely. Nothing fancy. It just needs to say the lessons are for adults who can't swim.”

“Does it say that now?” Syd asked.

“Somewhere. It starts with our journey.”

Oliver laughed.

“We had a woman ring last week,” Nora said. “Sixty-three. She asked if it was too late. I said, 'No ma'am, the class starts in 45 minutes.' She was asking if it was too late for her in life. How sad.”

Oliver looked down at the timetable.

“Anyways, she ended up coming to the class but she was like a half-hour late,” Nora said.

Oliver nodded knowingly and commented, “Apple po'.”

Nora agreed, said goodbye, and made her way towards the door.

Oliver put Nora's card in his pocket and picked up his name tag. He pressed it back onto his jacket as Syd carried their glasses to the bar.

They moved towards a table where someone was making room for them. The corner of Oliver's name tag lifted again.
