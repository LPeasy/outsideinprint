The stall opposite was gone. Only four pale marks remained where its table legs stood.

Syd checked the sales tally in his notebook.

**Syd:** Six.

**Oliver:** Six sold. Eighteen left.

Oliver folded a chair and leaned it against the tree behind them. Across the aisle, a man was pulling a rail of jackets towards a van.

Syd picked up the display copy. Its cover was slightly curled where people held it open.

**Syd:** The woman with the blue bag came back. She stood there reading for about ten minutes. I thought she was going to finish it and put it back.

**Oliver:** Could've charged her by the minute.

**Syd:** We'd have made more.

Oliver laughed. He lifted a carton onto the other chair and opened its flaps.

**Oliver:** I keep thinking about this AI consulting business, okay?

**Syd:** You've been thinking about it all day.

**Oliver:** Not all day. I spent a lot of the morning telling people how good your book was.

**Syd:** You did. Thank you.

**Oliver:** I meant it, too.

Syd put the display copy aside and began stacking the others.

**Oliver:** What would you need to sell for this to be your job?

**Syd:** More than six.

**Oliver:** I know that.

**Syd:** I'd need more books, probably. Somewhere people could find them. I'd have to keep coming out.

**Oliver:** Would you want to?

Syd looked along the row. Two women were taking down a canopy, holding its opposite corners.

**Syd:** I liked today. I wouldn't want every day to be today.

**Oliver:** That's what I'm thinking. If we could get some proper money coming in from something useful, you could keep doing this without needing every person who walked past to buy one.

**Syd:** What would you actually sell them?

**Oliver:** Like the thing I did for the man at work. Measurements in one email, delivery dates in another. Then somebody says, 'Ignore my last email.' I got it putting the details in one sheet, with links back. He still checks it. But it's all in front of him.

**Syd:** What did he pay you?

**Oliver:** Nothing. We work together. Two evenings to set it up. He asked me to change one of the columns last week. That was half an hour.

**Syd:** Would he pay for it?

**Oliver:** I haven't asked. I know he's using it. That's more than I knew two weeks ago.

Syd lifted a stack towards the carton. Oliver held the flap down.

**Syd:** And if he were paying you, he'd expect you to answer when he called.

**Oliver:** Yes. There'd be work. I'm offering to do some work.

**Syd:** You're also hoping it gets you out of doing so much work.

Oliver looked at the stack in Syd's hands.

**Oliver:** Are those going in?

Syd lowered them into the box.

**Oliver:** It feels like I'm building something for somebody else in my day job. I'm doing that already! I'd just like to choose who it's for.

**Syd:** I know. I think we can make bank doing it. I just keep thinking about the next book.

**Oliver:** We've still got eighteen of this one.

**Syd:** I know. I want to write another.

**Oliver:** So write it.

**Syd:** I'd sit down on Tuesday night and have somebody's sheet to fix.

**Oliver:** You rang me from the office on Tuesday. It was nearly eight.

**Syd:** I know! That's an evening gone already.

Syd moved the water bottle away from the books. A dark patch marked the tablecloth beneath it. He checked the bottom copy in the nearest stack.

**Syd:** These are fine.

He set the books inside. Oliver folded the damp corner of the cloth back on itself.

**Syd:** I think I had this idea that I was just gonna spend all my time building cool stuff, and then that was gonna work out.

**Oliver:** You got six people to buy one today.

**Syd:** With you standing there. I was letting them walk off.

**Oliver:** I nearly did that with the woman in the blue bag.

**Syd:** She already bought one. You were trying to sell her another.

**Oliver:** I didn't know she already paid you.

Syd laughed. They packed the last stack and laid the display copy on top.

**Oliver:** I'd like to try it while I've got something to offer. A couple of people. See if they think it's worth paying for.

**Syd:** I think if you could launch your AI consulting firm, I would join you. Straight up.

Oliver stopped flattening the carton flaps.

**Oliver:** After I've found the clients?

**Syd:** Yes.

**Oliver:** I could use some help before that.

**Syd:** I could give you Tuesday. We could work out who to ask.

**Oliver:** I thought you wanted to write.

**Syd:** I do. I can help you for an evening.

Syd pocketed the notebook. They cleared the table and carried it and the chairs to the car. When they came back, Syd lifted the carton. Oliver took the cloth and bottle.

**Oliver:** About seven on Tuesday?

**Syd:** Half seven. Let me get home first.
