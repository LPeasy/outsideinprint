The clock above the paint counter said ten to six. The mixer rattled, then stopped.

Syd kept a hand on the plywood lying across their flatbed cart. Two brushes lay beside its handle. Oliver set a tin of white primer on the board and spread three color cards along the counter.

**Oliver:** Red, blue, green. What do you think?

**Syd:** What goes where?

**Oliver:** One for the background. One for the letters.

**Syd:** Which one's which?

**Oliver:** We'll work that out. You pick your color, I'll pick my color.

He pushed the cards toward Syd.

**Oliver:** You get to pick first. Player one, go ahead.

Syd held the blue against the board. The plywood had a pale patch where its stock label came loose.

**Syd:** I haven't agreed! I haven't agreed to have it be different colors yet.

**Oliver:** You want the letters the same color as the background?

**Syd:** I thought we might leave the wood showing. Just paint BOOKS on it.

**Oliver:** We could. I thought we'd both choose something.

**Syd:** I just haven't thought about it yet.

**Oliver:** Well, have a look.

The clerk came back and glanced at the primer.

“That's the one you want for the board,” she said. “What were you thinking for the topcoat?”

“We haven't thought about it yet,” Oliver said.

“All right.”

She turned to the man waiting behind them. He lifted a tin onto the counter with both hands.

**Syd:** We could have a bit of red.

**Oliver:** Fine.

**Syd:** Maybe blue.

**Oliver:** Okay, maybe.

**Syd:** What if I just pick both colors?

**Oliver:** Uh, no, that's not how this works, my friend.

**Syd:** Green.

**Oliver:** Green? That will not look good.

**Syd:** I didn't say it would look good.

Oliver laughed and took the green card from him.

**Oliver:** I'll give you a choice. Red and blue or blue and green.

**Syd:** Not red and green?

**Oliver:** What is this, Christmas?

**Syd:** So blue and something.

**Oliver:** I would quite like blue, yes.

**Syd:** Okay! That's a great start.

The man behind them reached past Oliver for a stir stick. Oliver gathered the cards against his chest.

The clerk took the lid off the man's tin. She looked at the cards.

“Do you know what size tin you need?”

“Not sure...” Syd muttered. He looked over the tins.

Oliver glanced at the clock.

They rolled the cart to the end of the counter. One wheel caught on a torn corner of the floor mat. Syd lifted the front while Oliver pulled the mat straight.

**Oliver:** That's why I'm trying to get it sorted. This lady wants to go home. And I was giving you first choice as a courtesy. A sign of respect.

Syd looked at the three cards Oliver still held against his shirt.

**Syd:** I thought you handed me a job.

**Oliver:** Picking a color?

**Syd:** Picking it immediately!

**Oliver:** You can say you don't know.

**Syd:** I did.

The mixer started again. The motor whirred.

**Oliver:** I'm just joking around.

**Syd:** I know. You keep asking, though.

**Oliver:** I'm not trying to make you do anything.

**Syd:** No. But you're standing there with those cards.

He gestured wildly at the color cards.

Oliver looked down, then put the cards on the plywood.

**Oliver:** There.

**Syd:** Thank you.

**Oliver:** We do need paint. The stall's on Saturday. It's Thursday.

**Syd:** We've got tomorrow.

**Oliver:** You've got tomorrow. I'm working until five.

Syd rested his forearms on the handle of the cart.

**Oliver:** I don't mind making it. But if we come here together to choose the stuff, I don't want to get a message tomorrow saying you don't like the paint I've bought.

**Syd:** I wouldn't do that.

Oliver waited.

**Syd:** All right. I might.

Syd turned the blue card over.

Oliver rubbed a spot of dried paint off the counter with his thumbnail.

An announcement came over the ceiling speaker. Five minutes until closing.

Syd looked at the green card.

**Oliver:** Do you want green?

**Syd:** No.

Syd picked up the primer.

**Syd:** I'll leave this closed till we've decided.

**Oliver:** I'd like blue.

Oliver went over to talk to the clerk.

“We'll just take the primer tonight,” he said.

They paid for the plywood, primer, and two brushes. Syd tucked the color cards into his coat while Oliver pushed the cart through the doors.

Outside, the wind lifted one end of the receipt out of the bag. Syd caught it against the primer tin.

They set the bag on the ground beside the car and stood the board on its long edge. Oliver held it while Syd opened the hatch.

Syd took out the three cards. Under the light above their parking space, the blue looked almost black. He held it against the plywood, then put the red beside it.

Oliver moved his hand out of the way.
