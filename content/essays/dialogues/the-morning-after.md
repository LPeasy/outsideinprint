The café served breakfast until noon. At eleven forty-five, a waitress began replacing the menus on the empty tables.

Prince played through a speaker above the bar. Oliver was trying to open a packet of butter. Syd had pushed his plate aside to make room for his phone.

A young couple sat in the next booth. The woman had both hands around a glass of water.

**Syd:** This is a wild sentence.

**Oliver:** Read it.

**Syd:** “Deep-pocketed U.S. tech firms compete with governments for investors’ capital.”

Oliver abandoned the butter packet.

**Oliver:** Yup. Pretty wild.

**Syd:** Countries. They’re competing with countries.

**Oliver:** True though. The so-called hyperscalers have raised a staggering amount of cash during the AI boom. For data centers and whatnot.

**Syd:** And whatnot.

**Oliver:** I’m not reading you a prospectus.

**Syd:** Is there any analogue from history for this?

**Oliver:** Hmm. Not sure. Certainly no perfect analogy.

Syd passed him a knife.

**Oliver:** The massive productivity boom in the nineties? From new technologies.

**Syd:** Producing what? Software mostly, I think.

**Oliver:** But that was fundamentally different. The U.S. more or less had a balanced budget at the time. Last balanced federal budget was 1999, I think.

**Syd:** Poetic.

**Oliver:** The budget?

Syd pointed toward the speaker. Oliver listened.

**Oliver:** Oh. Right.

**Syd:** Prince was the goat.

**Oliver:** Truly.

The waitress passed with a tray of Bloody Marys. A celery stalk brushed the back of Oliver’s head.

“Sorry.”

**Oliver:** That’s all right.

Syd waited until she was gone.

**Syd:** You’ve got something in your hair.

Oliver put a hand up.

**Syd:** No. You’re fine.

**Oliver:** Thank you.

At the next table, the young woman leaned forward.

“For whatever it’s worth, he is a human just like the rest of us and he has regrets.”

The young man nodded.

“He really admires you,” she added.

“There’s no need for any hard feelings! Everything’s good,” he said.

“I know! Just putting it out there.”

A blender started behind the bar. Oliver moved his plate away from the edge of the table.

**Oliver:** The Industrial Revolution and the agricultural revolution are also far from perfect analogies. Some similarities. A bunch of key differences.

**Syd:** Closest thing to the data centers is the railroads and maybe U.S. Steel.

**Oliver:** Yeah. That makes sense.

**Syd:** But U.S. Steel wasn’t competing with Europe for bond sales. I don’t think.

**Oliver:** Keep the last part attached to that.

**Syd:** I guess the initial deal was pretty big between J.P. Morgan and Carnegie.

**Oliver:** A massive deal. But you’re still talking about a different situation. A much smaller federal government.

**Syd:** I know. I’m trying to find something close.

**Oliver:** Close in what way?

**Syd:** Something being built that changes everything around it. So much money going into it that you start wondering who’s actually in charge.

Oliver took a bite of toast.

**Oliver:** Those are two different questions.

**Syd:** They seem related.

**Oliver:** They might be. Which one bothers you?

Syd looked down at the sentence again.

**Syd:** I think the tech companies are de facto more powerful than the U.S. federal government.

**Oliver:** More powerful at doing what?

**Syd:** Getting something done. ’Cause they have unified purposes within their organizations.

**Oliver:** And the government is supposed to have whose purpose?

**Syd:** Ours, presumably.

**Oliver:** Yours and mine?

**Syd:** That would simplify things.

**Oliver:** We couldn’t agree where to eat.

**Syd:** Yet here we are.

**Oliver:** You followed me inside.

**Syd:** Strong leadership.

Oliver reached for his coffee.

**Oliver:** I’m serious. They might find it easier to agree because they’re trying to do fewer things for fewer people. That doesn’t settle which one is more powerful.

**Syd:** Yeah. Good point. But knowing what you want ought to count for something.

**Oliver:** It does. What do they want?

He moved his cup so Syd could pull his plate back toward him.

**Syd:** That purpose being the profit motive, I guess, ultimately.

**Oliver:** That does help narrow it down.

**Syd:** I don’t know. I think Musk has a compelling vision. Not sure what Bezos actually wants for humanity other than making life easier and more convenient.

**Oliver:** And making boatloads of money.

**Syd:** Which, like, fair enough.

**Oliver:** Fair enough, I suppose.

Syd cut into his eggs.

**Syd:** I like the idea, but it gives me big WALL-E vibes.

**Oliver:** You don’t like convenience?

**Syd:** I love convenience. That’s what worries me.

**Oliver:** You could still get out of the chair.

**Syd:** It’s a very good chair.

**Oliver:** Then I suppose you’ll have to decide whether you want to get up.

**Syd:** I’d prefer a less demanding future.

Oliver laughed into his cup.

For a while they ate. The waitress carried a stack of lunch menus past them and stopped to take an order at the bar. The couple’s voices had dropped beneath the music.

**Oliver:** I don’t know what I think is going to happen.

**Syd:** I think the party keeps going.

**Oliver:** For now.

**Syd:** Exactly.

**Oliver:** That covers a lot of ground.

**Syd:** I’m leaving myself room.

**Oliver:** I noticed.

Syd checked the time on his phone.

**Syd:** Only way out is a jubilee.

**Oliver:** Well. We shall see.

**Syd:** A bond market, real estate, and population crash. About twenty years out.

Oliver put down his cup.

**Oliver:** That’s what you’re calling a jubilee?

**Syd:** Not much of a celebration, I guess.

**Oliver:** You think all three together?

**Syd:** I can imagine them going together. I don’t know what starts it.

**Oliver:** Or when.

**Syd:** That’s why I said “about.”

**Oliver:** That little word is working pretty hard.

**Syd:** It’s carrying the whole forecast.

Oliver laughed. Syd pushed his phone farther from his plate.

**Oliver:** What happens to the people who were counting on being paid?

**Syd:** In the crash?

**Oliver:** In the jubilee. Whatever we’re calling it.

**Syd:** I don’t suppose they’ll like the name either.

**Oliver:** No.

**Syd:** I’m not proposing it. I’m trying to picture what happens when people can’t keep their promises.

**Oliver:** Some can’t. Some won’t.

**Syd:** Doesn’t look much different to the person waiting.

Oliver turned his cup on its saucer.

The waitress returned with the coffee pot.

“Still doing all right?”

**Oliver:** Yes, thank you.

Syd held his cup out too. She filled both and went back to the bar.

**Syd:** You think we just keep going?

**Oliver:** I think people keep trying things. Some of them might work. We’ve given the crash twenty years. That’s a long time for nothing else to happen.

**Syd:** Fair point.

**Oliver:** You were interested in what gets built five minutes ago.

**Syd:** I still am. I don’t think that part stops.

Oliver pulled a piece of crust loose from his toast.

**Oliver:** But yeah. I think the party keeps going for a while. Beyond the current bond turbulence.

**Syd:** So we agree.

**Oliver:** About the party continuing. Not necessarily the rest of your itinerary.

**Syd:** Fine.

**Oliver:** It’s been quite a party. Gonna be a hell of a hangover.

**Syd:** I think we’re already in the hangover.

**Oliver:** Already?

**Syd:** Since around ’07, ’08.

**Oliver:** Ha. You could be right.

**Syd:** It’s the morning after and we’re pounding Bloody Marys.

Oliver looked toward the bar. The woman who had ordered the tray was picking an olive off a skewer. Her friends were trying to get everyone into a photograph.

**Oliver:** They do look pretty happy.

**Syd:** I’m sure they are.

The music stopped between songs. A chair scraped near the door.

“I just want you to know how loved and appreciated you are,” the young woman said.

“Thanks! I appreciate you too.”

She let go of the water glass.

“It’s all love from my people. If it’s not that vibe from your people, hopefully I can earn it back someday.”

The young man set down his fork.

“You are my people.”

Oliver had picked up his coffee. He held it for a moment, then looked back across the table.

Syd raised his own cup.

**Syd:** Bottoms up.

Oliver touched his cup to Syd’s.
