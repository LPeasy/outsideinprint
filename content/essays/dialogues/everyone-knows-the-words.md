The lunch special had been rubbed off the diner's board, leaving only the price. Near the restrooms, an old jukebox glowed behind cloudy glass. A man from the counter dropped in a quarter, pressed two buttons, and waited for the mechanism to stir.

The waitress stopped beside their booth.

“How are we doing?”

“Couldn't be better,” Oliver said.

She filled their cups and went to collect a plate from the window.

**Syd:** Couldn't?

**Oliver:** What?

**Syd:** Be better.

Oliver looked down at his coffee.

**Oliver:** I used to be able to do this.

**Syd:** Eat lunch?

**Oliver:** Talk without hearing myself act.

The man returned to the counter. Behind him, a record began with a little hiss.

**Syd:** She wasn't asking for an account of your life.

**Oliver:** I know that. It's the voice. I hear it come out and think, who is that for?

**Syd:** Her, presumably.

**Oliver:** Yes, but I've got one for everybody. Warm voice. Serious voice. The voice that says I'm doing well but would never be so vulgar as to mention it.

**Syd:** I've heard that one.

**Oliver:** I'm sure you have.

**Syd:** Not lately. You've canceled twice.

**Oliver:** I haven't been much company.

**Syd:** I was only asking you to eat.

**Oliver:** I'm here, aren't I?

Syd didn't answer.

**Oliver:** I went to that thing on Friday.

**Syd:** How was it?

**Oliver:** Couldn't have been better.

Syd waited.

**Oliver:** We were talking about a friend's essay. He wasn't there. Everyone said they liked it. Brave, honest, all of that. Then an editor came over and said he thought it was a little thin. Within five minutes, people were explaining why they'd never thought much of it.

**Syd:** Perhaps he noticed something they hadn't.

**Oliver:** He said it was thin. That was the whole contribution. Someone who'd just called the ending brave started calling it self-indulgent.

**Syd:** Those aren't necessarily opposites.

**Oliver:** I'm aware. But I had a sentence ready too. About how we'd mistaken confidence for depth.

**Syd:** Did you think that?

**Oliver:** I don't know. I knew it would work.

The waitress set down their plates. Syd moved his cup to make room for his sandwich. Oliver lifted the toast off his eggs.

**Syd:** Did you say it?

**Oliver:** No. I said I liked the ending.

**Syd:** And they let you live.

**Oliver:** Someone nodded. We talked about something else. I wasn't expecting them to kill me.

**Syd:** You make it sound dangerous.

**Oliver:** No. Easy. That's what bothered me. I could have said it and gone on enjoying myself.

At the counter, the waitress called a regular “sweetheart,” then told him to move his newspaper because she wasn't putting his soup on the sports page.

**Oliver:** Now I watch people watching each other. Who's laughing. Who's allowed to interrupt. After a while I can hardly hear what anyone is saying.

**Syd:** Including anyone you might like.

**Oliver:** I used to like all of them. I still want to. Then I hear that voice and I want to get out of the room.

**Syd:** Hers?

**Oliver:** Everybody's. Mine. I know she isn't doing what they were doing.

**Syd:** She's glad to see him. She'd also like him to move his paper.

**Oliver:** I haven't accused her of anything.

**Syd:** You keep looking over there.

**Oliver:** She keeps talking. It's a diner.

Syd reached for the ketchup. It was nearly empty. He held it over his plate and waited.

**Syd:** Is this the Jung you've been reading?

**Oliver:** Some of it. The persona. The face you learn to wear around other people. I'm starting to wonder how much of my character is just knowing what people expect.

**Syd:** And now you don't know?

**Oliver:** I still know. I can't stop knowing. But I don't want to spend the rest of my life doing it.

**Syd:** Doing what? Being different with different people? You don't talk to me the way you talk to your mother.

**Oliver:** Nor should I. The problem is mistaking the part for the whole person. Individuation means becoming more fully yourself. That includes coming to terms with parts you don't yet know. Not just the ones you'd choose.

**Syd:** Does that mean the friendly part wasn't you?

**Oliver:** No. I've been generous. I've also agreed to things because I was afraid to say no. Then I resented people for expecting me to do them.

**Syd:** From their end, you said yes.

**Oliver:** I know. But now I'll start to offer something and wonder whether I mean it. And by then I've missed whatever the person said next.

Syd tapped the bottle. It spat ketchup onto his cuff.

**Syd:** There it is.

**Oliver:** What?

**Syd:** The ketchup. Keep going.

Oliver passed him a napkin.

**Oliver:** I ought to know the difference between keeping something private and having no idea what I think until I know who's asking.

**Syd:** I don't see why you have to stay home to do it.

**Oliver:** Because when I'm alone I can hear myself. I'm not waiting for someone to approve of every little thing. I don't need ~

He stopped.

Syd looked up from his sleeve.

**Oliver:** I had a whole speech about this on the way over.

**Syd:** You were doing well.

**Oliver:** That's what I wanted you to think.

Syd put the napkin down.

**Oliver:** I knew where you'd laugh. I even had your answer ready.

**Syd:** Was it a good one?

**Oliver:** It let me keep talking.

A spatula scraped across the grill.

**Syd:** So don't give me the speech.

Oliver opened his mouth. Syd waited, then went back to his sandwich.

**Oliver:** I don't know what to say now.

**Syd:** You can eat. You haven't touched those.

Oliver cut into his eggs.

**Oliver:** I wanted that editor to like me.

**Syd:** You'd like to be published.

**Oliver:** It wasn't only that. I wanted him to think I was interesting. I wanted everyone there to think so. And when I left, I hoped somebody would notice I'd gone.

**Syd:** Well, I noticed.

**Oliver:** You weren't there.

**Syd:** No. I mean here.

Oliver looked at him.

**Syd:** I don't know what you want me to do. Keep asking? Leave you alone? I get a message saying next week, then next week comes and you're busy again.

**Oliver:** I haven't been busy.

**Syd:** I know.

The waitress came for the empty ketchup bottle. She looked at Syd's sleeve, brought him a damp cloth, and took an order from the booth behind them.

**Oliver:** I thought you wouldn't want to sit through this.

**Syd:** I might not. You could let me find out.

**Oliver:** You could have asked whether I was avoiding you.

**Syd:** I was hoping you'd rule me out before I had to.

Oliver pushed his toast through the egg on his plate.

**Oliver:** I don't want you to stop asking.

**Syd:** Then don't keep putting me off.

They ate. At the counter, the regular laid his newspaper across an empty stool. The waitress took it off so a woman could sit down.

**Oliver:** That's the part I didn't want to find. How much I wanted their approval. I think that's part of what Jung means by the shadow. I could see it in them before I'd admit it in myself.

**Syd:** Maybe that's what you couldn't stand about them.

**Oliver:** My wanting their approval doesn't make what they did honest.

**Syd:** No.

**Oliver:** Then don't explain them away by explaining me.

**Syd:** I'm not trying to.

**Oliver:** You were getting comfortable.

Syd leaned back. The vinyl seam beneath his shoulder gave a small squeak.

**Syd:** I think you were right to say you liked the essay. I don't think everybody who smiles at you is doing what those people did on Friday.

**Oliver:** Neither do I.

**Syd:** You've put them in the same conversation.

**Oliver:** Because I'm trying to tell you how it feels. I know there are differences. I don't feel them right now.

**Syd:** All right. But I like coming here. I like that she asks after me and remembers what I order. I don't want to wonder whether I should believe her every time she comes over.

**Oliver:** Neither do I.

The jukebox changed records again. “Downtown” came through the speaker at the bottom, louder than the song before it.

Oliver looked up.

**Syd:** What?

**Oliver:** Nothing. I haven't heard this in years.

His foot moved under the table.

**Syd:** You like it.

**Oliver:** I do.

**Syd:** Wouldn't have guessed.

**Oliver:** My mother used to sing it when she cleaned the kitchen. She never knew the second verse. She'd get louder anyway.

He listened until the verse changed.

**Oliver:** There. That's the part.

Syd turned slightly toward the jukebox. The waitress was rolling knives and forks into paper napkins at the far end of the counter.

**Oliver:** I thought we meant something together. On Friday. For a few minutes, we did. Or I did.

**Syd:** Someone else might have.

**Oliver:** I know which ones changed what they'd said.

**Syd:** I wasn't talking about them.

Oliver listened to the record.

The waitress returned with the pot. Syd held out his cup. Oliver covered his with one hand.

“No more for me, thanks.”

She took the empty plates and left the check between them.

**Oliver:** I'm still not going back this Friday.

**Syd:** I didn't ask you to.

**Oliver:** I might call the friend who wrote the essay.

**Syd:** To tell him they didn't like it?

**Oliver:** To tell him I did.

The song ended while Oliver was looking for a quarter.

**Syd:** I think lunch is a little more than that.

Oliver put a bill beside the check and took the quarter to the jukebox.

He bent over the title cards. One had slipped down inside its holder. He found the song on the row above it, fed the quarter into the slot, and pressed the buttons.

Nothing happened at first. He waited.

By the time he returned, the record had started again.

**Syd:** You paid to hear the same one?

**Oliver:** We talked through half of it.

**Syd:** It's a terrible song.

**Oliver:** You'll survive three minutes.

Syd moved the sugar dispenser and pushed Oliver's coffee toward him.

Behind the glass, the record turned. When the chorus came, Oliver knew the words.
