The man behind the counter folded a pizza box against his chest and called a number. A rider lifted one hand, collected it, and worked it into an insulated bag already holding two others. His bicycle stood outside with one wheel against the glass.

Syd checked his phone. Nine minutes.

Oliver pulled two paper plates from the stack beside the napkins and handed him one.

**Syd:** What am I supposed to do with this?

**Oliver:** Be ready.

**Syd:** I'm hungry metaphorically and physically.

**Oliver:** Which one gets the plate?

**Syd:** Physically, I just wanna eat some food, you know?

**Oliver:** We ordered food.

**Syd:** I know.

He put the plate on the narrow shelf beneath the window. Outside, the rider pushed off with one foot before climbing onto the saddle.

**Oliver:** That really is something.

**Syd:** He nearly hit the bin.

**Oliver:** The whole thing. Somewhere, three people are sitting in their houses. None of them has spoken to that man. He has their dinner, their addresses, the money sorted out. You can literally summon a person from the ether to deliver you a pizza.

**Syd:** He came from the counter.

**Oliver:** You know what I mean.

The oven door opened. A man in a floury apron slid a peel beneath a pizza and turned it. Syd watched him until the door closed.

**Oliver:** You can hear almost any piece of music, too. All on that thing you're angry with because dinner is taking nine minutes.

**Syd:** I'm not angry with it.

**Oliver:** You've checked it four times.

**Syd:** It might have good news.

**Oliver:** It had good news when you ordered. You wanted a pizza. They agreed to make one.

Syd looked at the menu above the till.

**Syd:** Do you want fries?

**Oliver:** We've got a whole pizza coming.

**Syd:** I thought you were celebrating what was available.

**Oliver:** I could eat a couple.

**Syd:** But you can't order, like, two fries.

**Oliver:** A small portion.

**Syd:** That isn't two fries.

**Oliver:** You don't have to eat all of them.

**Syd:** Then why buy them?

**Oliver:** A small amount of something really good that's not so great for you is ideal.

The man at the till looked up.

“Two orders of fries?”

“Uh-uh, I didn't want two orders of fries, I only wanted two fries,” Oliver said.

“One portion?”

“No, thanks.”

Syd waited until the man turned away.

**Syd:** There. You wanted two, and you've got none.

**Oliver:** I was doing quite well until you got involved.

**Syd:** Can you actually leave the rest?

**Oliver:** Fries, yes.

**Syd:** Anything?

**Oliver:** Barbecue potato chips, no. I, I'm gonna eat the bag. You know what I mean?

**Syd:** So you don't buy them.

**Oliver:** Sometimes I buy them.

**Syd:** And then?

**Oliver:** Then I have a very good first ten minutes.

Syd laughed. Oliver moved the plates to let a woman reach the napkin dispenser. She took two, then came back for a third.

Syd's screen lit. The estimate now said twelve minutes. He held it out.

**Oliver:** I see it.

**Syd:** It was nine.

**Oliver:** It's an estimate.

**Syd:** The picture has been ready the whole time.

He turned the phone toward himself. The pizza in the photograph had a slice lifted out of it, cheese hanging between the slice and the rest.

**Syd:** Look at that. Somebody is already eating mine.

**Oliver:** You chose the picture.

**Syd:** I know. Now I'm standing in front of an oven looking at a photograph of something in the oven.

**Oliver:** You could put it away.

Syd slipped the phone into his coat.

**Syd:** There. Same appetite.

**Oliver:** I wasn't claiming to have fed you.

A second rider came through the door carrying his helmet. Oliver moved closer to Syd so the man could pass between them and the counter.

**Syd:** I am very grateful to be human.

**Oliver:** Yeah, I wouldn't pick anything else.

**Syd:** I like all this. Some evenings I don't want any of it.

The cook opened the oven again and drew out a pizza. Oliver watched him ease it onto the board.

**Oliver:** Sometimes I think we've got used to things we ought to be astonished by.

**Syd:** Sometimes I'm tired. I don't want to have to be astonished as well.

The wheel ran across the crust. The man at the till took a box from the stack.

“Thirty-one.”

Oliver lifted their receipt. He collected the box, set it on the shelf between their plates, and lifted the lid.

**Syd:** Dude, a pizza.

Oliver laughed and held the box while Syd worked a slice loose. The point sagged as he lifted it. He brought the paper plate underneath with his other hand and caught it.
