The ant went marching over the craggy wooden surface of the picnic table. He turned along the edge of, to him anyways, a vast cavern separating one two-by-four from another. At the edge, he waved his antennae back and forth over the gap.

Oliver noticed him just as he was preparing to take a bite of his sandwich.

He stopped.

Syd looked up from his phone.

**Syd:** What?

**Oliver:** Ant.

**Syd:** Where?

Oliver pointed.

The ant continued along the edge of the gap, stopped beside a crumb, and examined it.

Syd leaned over.

**Syd:** That's a nice one.

**Oliver:** Nice?

**Syd:** Healthy. Ambitious.

Oliver tore off a corner of bread and placed it a few inches away.

The ant ignored it.

**Syd:** There's your problem.

**Oliver:** What problem?

**Syd:** You've given him something for nothing. Now he doesn't want it.

**Oliver:** He's an ant.

**Syd:** Exactly.

They were sitting at a picnic table beside the river. A few children played near the swings, and across the lawn a man was trying to throw a tennis ball farther than his dog could run.

Syd put down his phone.

**Syd:** I think we're living through the most extraordinary period in human history.

**Oliver:** That's a large claim.

**Syd:** I think it's happening right now.

**Oliver:** What is?

**Syd:** The next great leap. Artificial intelligence. Scientific discovery. Mathematics, physics. All these problems we've been working on for decades.

**Oliver:** Have we solved them?

**Syd:** Some of them. We're getting closer on others. The machines can work on a problem for days without stopping.

**Oliver:** People can do that.

**Syd:** Not without stopping.

**Oliver:** No. We generally stop to eat.

He lifted his sandwich away from the ant.

**Syd:** Think about the tools we have already. I can turn on my air conditioner from right here.

**Oliver:** Congratulations.

**Syd:** No, think about it. My house is three miles away. I touch this little piece of glass and the temperature changes.

**Oliver:** Is it hot at your house?

**Syd:** Probably.

**Oliver:** Then turn it on.

Syd tapped the screen.

**Syd:** There. We basically have like telepathy.

**Oliver:** With an air conditioner.

**Syd:** With everything. We talk to people on the other side of the planet. We send machines across the ocean. We can watch a man standing on another continent while he watches us.

**Oliver:** We can also order sandwiches without leaving the house.

**Syd:** It's like indistinguishable from like magic.

**Oliver:** The sandwiches?

**Syd:** All of it!

Oliver looked at the sandwich in his hand.

**Oliver:** This one's pretty good.

The ant had found the piece of bread. It seized the crumb and began dragging it toward the gap between two boards.

Syd watched.

**Syd:** Look at that.

**Oliver:** He's taking it home.

**Syd:** He's got a logistics problem.

**Oliver:** He's got a piece of bread.

**Syd:** Same thing.

The ant reached the crack. The crumb was too large to fit through.

It pulled.

Nothing happened.

It turned and pushed.

Nothing happened.

**Oliver:** That's unfortunate.

**Syd:** He'll figure it out.

The ant abandoned the crumb and crossed the gap without it.

**Oliver:** Apparently not.

**Syd:** He's assessing the situation.

**Oliver:** From the other side?

**Syd:** Fresh perspective.

Oliver took a bite of his sandwich.

Syd picked up his phone again.

**Syd:** Here's what gets interesting. What happens when the machines start doing things on their own?

**Oliver:** What sort of things?

**Syd:** Making plans. Running businesses. Acquiring resources. Thousands of independent agents pursuing different goals.

**Oliver:** Who gives them the goals?

**Syd:** Somebody, originally.

**Oliver:** And afterward?

**Syd:** That's the question.

**Oliver:** You seem awfully pleased about it.

**Syd:** It's fascinating.

**Oliver:** You can be fascinated by something you'd rather not happen.

**Syd:** Sure. But they're not necessarily malevolent.

**Oliver:** No.

**Syd:** They're not necessarily benevolent.

**Oliver:** No.

**Syd:** But they're independent.

**Oliver:** Would they be?

**Syd:** Some of them might be.

**Oliver:** Then I'd like to know what they want.

**Syd:** What's the goal of a billionaire? Could be anything. Why would an intelligent machine be any different?

**Oliver:** I suppose I'd ask the billionaire.

**Syd:** And he'd tell you?

**Oliver:** Probably not.

**Syd:** There you go.

**Oliver:** That doesn't make him a mystery. It makes him uncooperative.

The ant returned to its crumb. It gripped the bread and began pulling it sideways along the crack.

Oliver watched.

**Oliver:** Suppose you're building a house.

**Syd:** All right.

**Oliver:** There's an anthill where you want to put the foundation.

**Syd:** Unfortunate.

**Oliver:** What do you do?

**Syd:** Move the anthill.

**Oliver:** How?

**Syd:** With a shovel.

**Oliver:** Without killing any ants?

Syd considered this.

**Syd:** Probably not.

**Oliver:** But you don't hate them.

**Syd:** No.

**Oliver:** You don't want to hurt them.

**Syd:** No.

**Oliver:** You just want to build your house.

**Syd:** Right.

Oliver sat back.

**Oliver:** Now suppose something much smarter than you wants to build something.

Syd smiled.

**Syd:** Ah.

**Oliver:** And you're where it wants to build.

**Syd:** Now we're getting somewhere.

**Oliver:** Are we?

**Syd:** That's the problem. It doesn't have to hate us.

**Oliver:** No.

**Syd:** It just needs a shovel.

They looked at the ant.

It had found a narrower place in the gap and was pulling the crumb through.

**Oliver:** There he goes.

**Syd:** Smart little fellow.

**Oliver:** You were ready to build a house on him a minute ago.

**Syd:** Hypothetically.

**Oliver:** Very reassuring.

Syd laughed.

The ant crossed onto the next board.

**Syd:** There's another possibility, though.

**Oliver:** What's that?

**Syd:** Maybe they like us.

**Oliver:** Why?

**Syd:** Why not?

**Oliver:** You can't answer every question with another question.

**Syd:** I can try.

Oliver waited.

**Syd:** Maybe intelligence leads to an appreciation for life. Maybe consciousness is valuable in itself.

**Oliver:** To whom?

**Syd:** To conscious beings.

**Oliver:** Including machines?

**Syd:** Maybe. I don't know what consciousness even means half the time.

**Oliver:** That seems worth sorting out.

**Syd:** Sure. But it's possible that the AIs could conclude that and then want to protect conscious observers, i.e., us.

**Oliver:** That would be nice.

**Syd:** Wouldn't it?

**Oliver:** Provided they let us do something.

**Syd:** What do you mean?

**Oliver:** Suppose they decide we're too important to risk losing. So they take care of us. Feed us, house us, keep us safe.

**Syd:** Sounds comfortable.

**Oliver:** Very.

**Syd:** What's wrong with that?

**Oliver:** Well, what if I want to go somewhere?

**Syd:** Where?

**Oliver:** Anywhere.

**Syd:** Why?

**Oliver:** Because I want to.

**Syd:** You sound like a child.

**Oliver:** Perhaps.

The ant had reached the edge of the table. It stopped with its crumb, feeling along the rim.

Below it was a considerable drop.

Oliver held out his hand.

**Oliver:** Come on, little fellow.

The ant turned away and started back along the rim.

**Syd:** He doesn't want your help.

**Oliver:** He doesn't know I'm helping.

**Syd:** Maybe he doesn't need you.

**Oliver:** He's about to fall off a table.

**Syd:** He's got a plan.

Oliver picked up his empty sandwich wrapper and held it beneath the ant.

The ant walked past it.

Oliver moved the wrapper again.

This time, the ant stepped onto the paper.

**Oliver:** There.

He carried it to the grass and lowered it beside a tree.

The ant stepped off, still dragging its crumb.

Oliver watched until it disappeared among the roots.

**Syd:** You might have just ruined his whole afternoon.

**Oliver:** I saved his life.

**Syd:** Maybe. Maybe he was trying to get to the other side of the table.

**Oliver:** There's plenty to explore over there.

**Syd:** That's not the point.

Oliver folded the wrapper.

**Oliver:** What's the point?

**Syd:** You decided where he ought to go.

Oliver looked toward the tree.

**Oliver:** I suppose I did.

He put the wrapper in the trash.

Across the lawn, the dog finally caught the tennis ball. It stood with the ball in its mouth while its owner called for it to come back.

The dog did not come back.

**Oliver:** You know, there's something strange about all this.

**Syd:** The dog?

**Oliver:** No. Everything you were saying about technology. We've made life so comfortable. We can summon almost anything we want. And now we're building machines to do the difficult thinking for us.

**Syd:** Pretty amazing.

**Oliver:** It is.

**Syd:** I can't imagine what could be more comfortable than being able to summon anything at any moment with a click of a button telepathically.

**Oliver:** And yet here we are.

**Syd:** Eating sandwiches?

**Oliver:** Talking about ants.

**Syd:** Good sandwiches.

**Oliver:** Very good sandwiches.

They watched the river for a while.

**Oliver:** Suppose you lived in a beautiful valley.

**Syd:** All right.

**Oliver:** Perfect weather. Plenty of food. No enemies. Everything you could possibly need.

**Syd:** Sounds wonderful.

**Oliver:** It is wonderful.

**Syd:** Good.

**Oliver:** And there's a mountain at the end of the valley.

**Syd:** Naturally.

**Oliver:** You've never been over it.

**Syd:** Okay.

**Oliver:** Wouldn't you want to know what's on the other side?

**Syd:** Of course.

**Oliver:** Why?

**Syd:** Because it's there.

Oliver smiled.

**Oliver:** Exactly.

**Syd:** Well, that's not entirely rational.

**Oliver:** Probably not.

**Syd:** Could be dangerous.

**Oliver:** Certainly.

**Syd:** Could be nothing over there.

**Oliver:** Could be.

Syd looked toward the tree where the ant had disappeared.

**Syd:** People have an inherent desire to explore and find things.

**Oliver:** Some people.

**Syd:** Some people. But you get enough of them together, somebody's going over that mountain.

**Oliver:** And somebody's going to tell him not to.

**Syd:** Probably for his own good.

Oliver laughed.

They gathered their things and started toward the parking lot.

Halfway there, Oliver stopped.

**Syd:** What?

Oliver pointed toward a narrow footpath disappearing into the trees beside the river.

**Oliver:** Ever been down there?

**Syd:** No.

**Oliver:** Wonder where it goes.

Syd reached for his phone.

Oliver looked at him.

Syd put it away.

**Syd:** All right.

They took the path.
