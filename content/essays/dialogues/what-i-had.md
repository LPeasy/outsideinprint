The pizza place had three picnic tables beside the gas station. A box fan stood in the kitchen doorway, blowing warm air at nobody.

Oliver had already eaten the tip of a slice when Syd came back across the lot. Syd put a packet of Zig-Zags on the table and sat down.

**Oliver:** Pizza’s getting cold.

**Syd:** Some guy stopped me outside the store. Needed gas to get home.

**Oliver:** You give him anything?

**Syd:** My last dollar.

**Oliver:** Where’s home?

**Syd:** Didn’t ask. I hope a dollar was enough to get him there.

Oliver glanced at the rolling papers.

**Oliver:** What’d those cost?

**Syd:** Four.

**Oliver:** Four dollars for yourself, one for the stranger.

**Syd:** I met myself first.

Oliver laughed.

**Syd:** I’d already bought them. I wasn’t standing there dividing up a charity budget.

**Oliver:** I’m not grading you.

**Syd:** You did announce the percentages.

**Oliver:** The amounts.

**Syd:** Much better.

A pickup pulled between the tables and the pumps. The driver had his window down and one hand drumming on the roof. They waited for him to pass.

**Oliver:** What’d he say?

**Syd:** Asked if I was sure. I said I was in the red anyway. Then he felt bad and tried to give it back.

**Oliver:** You could’ve said it was all you could spare.

**Syd:** It wasn’t spare.

**Oliver:** All right.

**Syd:** I didn’t think that would bother him. I was trying to say I wasn’t any better off. He didn’t have to explain himself to me.

**Oliver:** Did you tell him that?

**Syd:** No. I told him to take the dollar.

**Oliver:** You’re asking him to get a lot out of that dollar.

Syd reached into the pizza box.

**Syd:** It got awkward. I wanted him to take it so we could both leave.

**Oliver:** Did he?

**Syd:** Yeah. Said thanks about four times. I hope it helped.

Oliver looked toward the pumps.

**Oliver:** You could’ve come over here.

**Syd:** For what?

**Oliver:** I had some money.

**Syd:** I wasn’t giving away your money.

**Oliver:** You could’ve asked me.

**Syd:** You were eating.

**Oliver:** It’s pizza, Syd.

**Syd:** And he was already embarrassed. I didn’t want to bring him an audience.

**Oliver:** I could’ve walked over without making a speech.

**Syd:** You’re making one now.

**Oliver:** I was forty feet away.

Syd took a bite. Oliver left him to it.

At the next table, two women were sharing a pizza. One had picked the green peppers off her slices and arranged them on the lid. The other sat sideways on the bench, her jacket folded across her knees.

“He wasn’t my first love, but he received every type of love I had to give.”

Her friend stopped moving the peppers. A timer sounded inside the kitchen, and someone called for it to be turned off.

**Syd:** That’s a hell of a thing to be able to say.

**Oliver:** Yeah.

**Syd:** I hope he knew.

**Oliver:** I hope she told him.

**Syd:** You’ve got homework for everybody today.

Oliver smiled, but Syd kept looking at him.

**Syd:** Would you say something like that?

**Oliver:** To somebody I loved?

**Syd:** Knowing they might just say thanks.

**Oliver:** I’d want them to know.

**Syd:** That’s not all you’d want.

**Oliver:** No. I’d want them to love me back. I’d probably be pretty disappointed with thanks.

**Syd:** Four times, though. How would you feel about four thanks?

Oliver laughed and shook his head.

**Oliver:** I don’t know. I’d still rather say it.

The woman with the peppers slid the last slice onto her friend’s plate. Their voices dropped beneath the fan again.

**Syd:** I hate that you can give somebody something and then spend the rest of the day wondering whether it was enough.

**Oliver:** It might not be.

**Syd:** I’m aware.

**Oliver:** That doesn’t make it nothing.

**Syd:** You make it sound like I should’ve stayed until he had a full tank.

**Oliver:** I’m saying you could’ve found out what he needed.

**Syd:** He told me what he needed. I gave him what I had.

**Oliver:** And you had a friend sitting over here.

Syd set his slice down.

**Syd:** Is that what we’re doing now? Counting everything I didn’t give him?

**Oliver:** I’d have liked to be asked. We sit here talking about what we’d do if we could do something. This time there was somebody right over there.

**Syd:** And I did something.

**Oliver:** I know. I wasn’t trying to replace you.

**Syd:** Fine. Next time somebody asks me for a dollar, I’ll bring you over to supervise.

Oliver leaned back from the table.

**Oliver:** Don’t do that.

**Syd:** What?

**Oliver:** Tell me you’ll do something you don’t intend to do just to shut me up.

**Syd:** I wasn’t being serious.

**Oliver:** I am. You gotta say what you believe, cause if you say anything else, pretty soon you won’t believe anything.

Syd looked toward the store. The pickup was gone. A woman in a blue work shirt was wiping her windshield, reaching across the hood to get the far corner.

**Syd:** I think what I did was worth doing. Even if it wasn’t enough to get him home.

**Oliver:** So do I.

**Syd:** You have a funny way of saying it.

**Oliver:** I can think that and still think you could’ve asked me.

**Syd:** Including when your friend just wants to eat his pizza and feel good about his dollar.

**Oliver:** Apparently.

Syd laughed once, despite himself.

**Syd:** I wasn’t telling you so you’d think I was a good person.

**Oliver:** I know.

**Syd:** The guy looked surprised. That’s what stayed with me. He asked for something and then looked surprised when he got it. I don’t like that.

Oliver looked toward the store.

**Syd:** I don’t want to get good at walking past people. I already do it enough.

**Oliver:** Then why make asking me such a terrible idea?

**Syd:** Because I don’t know where it stops. I come get you. You give him five. He needs twenty. Do we go inside and start asking everybody buying cigarettes?

**Oliver:** Maybe we ask him where he’s going before we worry about all that.

**Syd:** And if he’s going a hundred miles?

Oliver rubbed a spot of grease off his thumb.

**Oliver:** I don’t know what I’d do.

**Syd:** Neither do I. I knew what to do with a dollar.

The man from the kitchen came out carrying two flattened boxes. He held them against his hip and switched off the fan.

“Gonna need these tables in a few minutes.”

**Oliver:** We’re nearly done.

“No rush.”

The man stayed in the doorway.

Syd looked at him, then at Oliver. Oliver folded the lid over the last slice.

**Syd:** I still hope it got him home.

**Oliver:** Me too.

Syd picked up the rolling papers and slipped them into his pocket.

The two women were leaving. One waited while the other went back for her jacket. Oliver dropped their empty cups in the bin.

**Syd:** Can you give me a ride?

Oliver picked up his keys.

**Oliver:** Yeah.
