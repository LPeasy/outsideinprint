Oliver parked outside Syd’s house and opened the trunk.

**Syd:** Thanks for coming out to the airport. I appreciate the ride.

**Oliver:** No problem. I’ll get the other bag.

Syd rolled his suitcase to the front door and searched his pockets. Oliver followed him up the path.

**Syd:** He’s going to have some words for us.

**Oliver:** I wasn’t on the trip.

**Syd:** Well, you can tell him that…

He found the key and opened the door a few inches.

**Syd:** Hello?

A tiger-striped cat with long white socks sat in the middle of the room. He was taking a bath on the rug, one foot in the air. He looked toward the door, sniffed, and then bent to his foot again.

Oliver followed Syd inside and pushed the door shut with his heel.

**Syd:** Hey, buddy. I’m home.

The cat spread his toes.

**Oliver:** Where do you want this?

**Syd:** By the couch, thanks!

Oliver put the bag down. Syd crouched beside the rug and held out his hand.

The cat licked between the second and third toes, rolled onto his side, and arched his back in a giant stretch. He yawned and smacked his lips.

**Syd:** Look at him. He’s got boots on his feet. Love him.

**Oliver:** Your neighbor said he was fine, didn’t she?

**Syd:** Yeah. She came by twice a day. Said he ate everything.

**Oliver:** Well, that’s good.

**Syd:** Yeah. I was worried about him.

The cat flopped over to his other side and made a peep.

**Syd:** The big floppa!

The cat made a series of noises that sounded suspiciously like, “Where have you been?”

**Oliver:** Your cat is a ham.

Syd sat on the ground next to the cat.

**Syd:** In the morning he comes up to me and he says, “Meow.”

**Oliver:** Ah, yes. Very cute.

**Syd:** No, not a regular meow. Like when you wake up and your voice is really deep.

He made a low, rough sound in his throat.

**Syd:** “Meow.”

The cat stood up and walked over to Syd. He gave Syd a headbutt and said, “Meow.”

**Syd:** Just like that! That’s how he talks to me.

Oliver took off his jacket, draped it over the back of the couch, and sat down. The cat walked around Syd twice, and then he brushed up against Oliver’s leg.

**Syd:** There he is ~ my little guy.

The cat walked back toward Syd, passed beneath his outstretched hand, and then began sniffing a wheel on the suitcase.

**Syd:** That’s been through the airport. You don’t want that.

Syd stood up and looked around the room.

**Syd:** He’s just making sure I know.

**Oliver:** Know what?

**Syd:** That I was gone four days! Much too long, in his opinion.

**Oliver:** You were worried he wasn’t all right?

**Syd:** I knew he would be okay. I still missed him, though.

Syd went into the kitchen. He checked the cat’s water bowl. The cat followed, brushed past his ankle, and stopped right in the way. He sat properly and expectantly. A single drop of drool dripped off one of his fangs.

**Syd:** Oh, I see what you’re after.

Oliver leaned forward to see around the doorway. Syd was opening a can of delicious wet cat food.

**Oliver:** Lil man is eating good tonight. Probably thinks you’ve been out hunting.

**Syd:** For canned tuna?

**Oliver:** You brought it home, didn’t you?

Syd emptied the tuna into the food bowl and set it on the floor. While the cat ate, he rinsed and refilled the water bowl, then set it down beside the food.

**Syd:** Look, dude, my flight got in late. Then there was traffic.

The cat looked up briefly, his mouth full of tuna.

**Oliver:** Are you explaining what took you so long to your cat?

**Syd:** I’m pretty sure he just likes to hear my voice, especially when I’ve been gone.

When the cat finished eating, Syd followed him back to the living room. Oliver leaned forward and held out his hand.

**Oliver:** Hello, there.

The cat sniffed his fingers, then walked past him to brush his face against the suitcase.

Syd laid the case flat on the rug and unzipped it.

He folded back the lid and lifted out his shoes. Before he could reach for anything else, the cat stepped into the case.

**Syd:** Oh, why thank you for the help, sir.

The cat nosed beneath a sleeve, turned twice, and lowered himself onto the clothes. One white foot hung over the edge.

Syd set the shoes aside.

The cat watched them closely, then tucked his chin down and purred softly.

Oliver knelt beside the suitcase and lifted out a book. He turned it over and read the back cover.

Syd began working a pair of trousers loose from along the edge.

The cat lifted his head and gave a long, rough meow.

Syd stopped pulling.

**Syd:** Oh, there’s more.

The cat sat up in the suitcase and meowed again, louder.

**Syd:** I know, buddy.

Three short meows followed.

**Syd:** Four days. I know, I know.

Oliver set the book beside the suitcase and sat back on his heels.

The cat stretched his neck toward Syd and let out a long cry.

**Syd:** Well, I couldn’t get home any faster. I came straight from the airport.

The cat started again before he finished.

**Syd:** Oliver came and got me. You can ask him.

The cat turned his head.

**Oliver:** I wasn’t on the trip.

Syd laughed. The cat turned back to him and resumed.

**Syd:** No, I’m listening. Go ahead.

He reached over and scratched beneath the cat’s chin. The cat stretched his neck, accepted the scratching, and kept meowing.

Oliver stood and collected his jacket.

**Oliver:** I’ll leave you two to it.

**Syd:** Thanks again for the ride. We should plan a trip soon!

The cat gave another loud cry.

Oliver let himself out. Syd sat down on the rug beside the open suitcase.

**Syd:** All right. What else?

The cat climbed into Syd’s lap and settled into a deep, cozy purr.
