The rising sun reaches across the quilt, coloring the squares in dappled light. He opens his eyes, stretches, and hears someone whistling out on the sidewalk.

He slept well. Now there's coffee to make.

*I go to sleep when I get in bed, and when I wake up, I get up.*

His feet find the cool floor.

In the kitchen, he opens the window while the coffee brews. He leans against the counter, enjoying the smell, and picks out his favorite cup.

*I love waking up. Mornings are awesome!*

He carries his coffee and a book onto the porch. The chair gives its familiar creak as he sits. He stretches his legs into the sun and takes a sip.

For a while, the book rests on his knee.

*I deeply appreciate where I am.*

A neighbor comes along the sidewalk carrying a paper bag. She slows at his gate.

“Something smells good.”

“Coffee,” he says. “What have you got?”

“I walked over to the bakery.”

“And?”

She opens the bag for him to see.

A large cinnamon roll sits inside, its icing stuck to the paper, steam rising.

“The baker had just taken them out.”

He puts his book on the little table and points to the other chair.

“Coffee?”

“Please! We can split this.”

He goes inside for another cup and a plate.

*I get out of the way, and the blessings come pouring in!*

When he returns, she has pulled her chair up to the table. She breaks the roll in two, and they each take a piece. The middle is still warm.

She tells him about a train trip she is taking next month to see her sister.

“Does she know you’re coming?”

“She bought the ticket.”

He laughs.

“She must be looking forward to it.”

“She’s already told me what we’re having for supper.”

He asks what that will be. Soon they are talking about her sister’s cooking, then their own, then the biscuits he made last Sunday.

“How were they?”

“Good. I ate four standing at the counter.”

“Then next Sunday, I’ll bring the coffee.”

He takes another bite and listens as she tells him how their mother used to make biscuits. He wants to hear this part. He brings the pot outside so he won’t have to get up again.

“Little more?”

She slides her cup toward him.
