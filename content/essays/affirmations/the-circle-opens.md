At eight, I put a clean sheet of paper on the last chair.

Eight chairs form a circle in the library meeting room. Morning light lies across the tile. A cup of pens waits on a table by the door. I take one and sit opposite the window.

The chair holds me. I rest the pen across my open palms.

“I am at peace. I’m safe in my own body.”

A book cart passes in the hall. Its wheels click over the seams in the tile. I feel the floor beneath my shoes and let my shoulders rest against the chair.

“I feel no pain and no discomfort.”

The door opens. A woman with a canvas bag chooses the seat in the sun. Others arrive with coats folded over their arms. The room fills with the scrape of chair legs and the sound of paper lifted from seats.

I greet each person. When the room grows quiet, my voice is ready.

<figure class="franklin-pullquote" aria-label="Affirmation">
  <blockquote>I don’t feel anxiety. What I feel is readiness.</blockquote>
  <figcaption>- Robby V.</figcaption>
</figure>

The woman by the window raises her page. “Where do we begin?”

“Where you are,” I say. “Choose one thing you can see.”

She looks at the light across her shoes. A man beside me turns his pen in his hand. I look at the bright edge of the page against my trousers.

“Clear mind, strong heart.”

A pen touches paper, lifts, then touches again. The hand around it softens.

“We don’t resist, we relax.”

I say it to the circle. A few people smile and bend over their pages. My own pen moves. I write down the sound of the cart wheels crossing tile.

By the time I reach the end of the line, pens are moving all around me.
