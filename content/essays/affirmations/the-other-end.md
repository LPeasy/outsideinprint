The dresser waits at the curb in a square of morning sun.

Honey-colored oak. Six wide drawers. Brass pulls worn smooth.

The woman two blocks over has tied the drawers shut with blue cotton cord. Together, we settle it onto my hand truck, and I guide it home.

The wheels answer each seam in the sidewalk with a low wooden knock.

At my building, I tip the dresser upright.

Two flights.

One narrow landing.

A whole building moving through its morning.

“The universe works for us.”

I lean the hand truck toward the first step.

Before the wheel rises, somebody takes the top corner.

A neighbor from the first floor stands beside me. He sets his coffee on the radiator and plants one palm against the oak.

“I’ve got this end.”

He sees where the weight continues and places his hands there.

  <blockquote>People always help us without us having to ask.</blockquote>
  <figcaption>- Robby V.</figcaption>
</figure>

The sentence lives in the pressure easing through my arms.

A neighbor on the landing lifts the blue cord away from the wheel and folds the stair runner back from the turn.

She looks at the dresser. Then at us.

“Bring the top toward me.”

Everyone sees the next useful thing.

We lift.

The dresser rises one step at a time.

Rubber wheels press against stone. The brass pulls catch morning light. Our shoes find the same slow rhythm.

At the first landing, we rest the dresser on the hand truck and study the turn.

A third neighbor reaches through the rail and takes the far corner.

I laugh.

“The cavalry is here to help! Our friends and families are all around us ready to provide support.”

The words are grand. The work is plain and beautiful: palms against oak, knees bent, careful voices, one shared weight.

*The other end keeps changing hands. The help stays.*

“Easy.”

“Toward me.”

“Now lift.”

The dresser turns.

For a moment, my view is full of wood. I follow the pressure through the handles and the voices above me.

Every hand does something different. One purpose moves through all of them.

The other end keeps changing hands. The help stays.

On the next flight, the neighbor with the coffee passes his corner to the woman upstairs. She receives it smoothly.

Each of us owns one useful part.

I guide the wheels. One hand steadies the top. Another keeps the dresser clear of the rail.

The narrow stairs carry us upward, one inch at a time.

Inside my apartment, we lower the dresser against the wall where sunlight crosses the floor.

The blue cord loosens. I pull it free and open the top drawer. Wood slides on wood with a soft hush.

Everyone stands around it for one quiet second.

“There’s so many people in our lives who love us and will always be there for us.”

The sentence widens beyond the room: family, friends, neighbors, every person who recognizes the other end of a weight.

I thank them.

One collects his coffee. One smooths the runner. One heads back upstairs.

The dresser is mine.

The lift belongs to all of us.

I coil the blue cord and place it in the drawer. The wood holds the warmth of our hands.

Support has a sound: rubber wheels, a voice calling the turn, laughter on the landing.

I rest my palm on the oak.

“The universe works for us.”

The next weight may belong to someone else.

My place is ready.

The other end.
