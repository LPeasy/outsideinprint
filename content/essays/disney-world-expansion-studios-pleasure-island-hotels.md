*Brands, Business, and Consumer Choice · Disney World Development, Part Three: A Longer Stay*

*[The Florida Project](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/) followed Walt's plans and Roy's work bringing the Florida resort to opening. [The EPCOT Project](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/) traced the proposed city's evolution into the second park. This installment follows the studio park, evening entertainment, and hotels that enlarged the vacation around them.*

In the summer of 1988, the cameras were rolling at Disney's Florida studio while builders worked on the visitor park beside it. Three soundstages were in use. *Win, Lose or Draw* and *Siskel & Ebert* were among the productions described in the [September studio newspaper](https://orlandomemory.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Disney_MGM_Studio_News1.pdf#page=1). A June showcase hosted invited guests. August aerial photographs showed the park taking shape around the production facilities.

![An August 1988 aerial photograph shows Disney-MGM Studios production buildings, its mouse-eared water tower, and unfinished visitor areas.](oip-image:essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/studios-construction-1988)

*The studio and visitor attraction taking shape together, August 1988. Photograph published in [Disney-MGM Studios News, September 1988, p. 4](https://orlandomemory.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Disney_MGM_Studio_News1.pdf#page=2). © The Walt Disney Company.*

In his [1987 shareholder letter](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1987/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281987%29.pdf#page=5), company chairman Michael Eisner set out the next stages of Disney's growth program. Grand Floridian, Caribbean Beach, Pleasure Island, and an EPCOT addition were expected to increase the Florida resort's appeal and revenue. The studio tour was planned for 1989.

The program reached across the holiday ~ another park for the day, rooms at different prices for the night, and an evening district after dinner. Disney was giving visitors more reasons to stay. The customers would decide whether another night with the mouse was worth the bill.

## Disney-MGM Studios: Planning Disney World's Third Park

The plans behind that construction were already public by 1985. Disney's [annual report](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1985/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201985.pdf#page=8) described a separately gated complex with themed entertainment and facilities for live-action and animation production. The company intended the studio to support its expanding film and television work.

The MGM name supplied a familiar Hollywood identity. Disney licensed the name and film-library material for logos, excerpts, and memorabilia. Its report made the ownership distinction explicit ~ "MGM is not a partner in the studio tour attraction."

Sears joined through a different agreement. The [1987 report](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1987/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281987%29.pdf#page=4) announced a ten-year alliance involving merchandise, promotion of Disney films, and sponsorship of studio-tour attractions. Elsewhere, Disney named Sears as presenter of a [proposed ride through movie scenes](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1987/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281987%29.pdf#page=8).

[EPCOT also brought outside companies into the visitor experience](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/#how-disney-financed-epcot-center). Here, MGM supplied licensed material and Sears supplied sponsorship and promotion. The published arrangements gave them distinct roles, without making either company an owner of the park.

The working studio gave the project another purpose. Disney's [production brochure](https://orlandomemory.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Studio_Open_Brochure.pdf#page=6) offered three soundstages totaling 27,200 square feet, along with sets and production support.

## Grand Floridian and Caribbean Beach Open in 1988

The hotel program widened the choice of accommodations. Disney's [1987 property account](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1987/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281987%29.pdf#page=54) distinguished three company-owned and operated resort hotels from seven Hotel Plaza hotels on leased land. Villas and campsites appeared separately. Even before the next wave of construction, a room on Disney property could involve different owners and operators.

The new hotels would serve different parts of that market. The company described Caribbean Beach as a response to demand for [moderately priced lodging](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1987/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281987%29.pdf#page=30). Grand Floridian would offer a Victorian resort setting.

Grand Floridian [opened in June 1988, followed by Caribbean Beach's initial opening in October](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1988/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281988%29.pdf#page=10). Disney reported 900 rooms and a $120 million cost for Grand Floridian. Caribbean Beach began receiving guests as part of a phased, [2,100-room project then estimated at $135 million](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1988/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281988%29.pdf#page=40). Disney reported its [completion the following year](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=10).

![Grand Floridian Beach Resort buildings with white balconies and red roofs stand behind a swimming pool in a photograph published in 1988.](oip-image:essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/grand-floridian-1988)

*Grand Floridian Beach Resort in Disney's [1988 annual report, p. 10](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1988/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281988%29.pdf#page=12). Photograph: The Walt Disney Company.*

The balconies, rooflines, pools, and grounds were part of the purchase. Guests could visit the same park and return to very different hotels that evening.

Disney was selling time as well as rooms. A [summer 1989 advertisement](https://archive.org/download/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol24.3_1989.Summer/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol24.3_1989_Summer.pdf#page=16) suggested at least four days for the three parks and proposed staying a week. Its limited Disney Inn or Village Resort Club Suites offer included breakfast at $59 per person per night, plus tax, based on double occupancy ~ $118 for two. The offer covered August 20, 1989, through February 8, 1990, excluding holidays, with limited rooms available.

[The original Florida resort](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/#what-opened-in-1971) joined attractions to lodging and transportation. The new rooms broadened a familiar choice ~ where to sleep, what to spend, and whether another night would be worth it.

## Disney-MGM Studios Opens on May 1, 1989

On May 1, 1989, Disney-MGM Studios opened to the public. Disney reported that it stopped admitting guests [thirty minutes after the 9 a.m. opening](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=10).

During the opening year, [The Great Movie Ride](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=13) carried riders through [recreated scenes of Gene Kelly singing in the rain and Judy Garland following the yellow brick road](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=12). Elsewhere, guests could watch parts of actual production or appear in television sequences through [Superstar Television](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=12).

Production operations began in [February 1988](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1988/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281988%29.pdf#page=10). The public attraction followed more than a year later. Working soundstages and recreated movie scenes now belonged to the same visit.

Disney reported a [project cost above $500 million](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=46). The park was open, and Disney now had to bring in enough paying guests to make the investment worthwhile.

## Pleasure Island: The 1989 Opening and Nightly New Year's Eve

Pleasure Island gave the expansion an evening destination, with shops, food, music, comedy, and clubs. The complex operating in 1989 appeared to occupy an old industrial waterfront. Disney wrote that past for the occasion.

Disney's [1988 account of the design](https://disneydollars.net/index_html_files/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol23.3_1988_Summer.pdf#page=16) explained the invention. Merriweather Adam Pleasure, the sailmaking magnate whose supposed industrial past gave the place its setting, belonged to a Disney legend. Imagineers used the buildings and props to give visitors pieces of that invented history.

The plans covered several kinds of evenings. Videopolis was intended for teenagers. The RollerDrome would offer skating. The Adventurers Club and Comedy Warehouse would welcome accompanied children, while other clubs were directed toward adults. Imagineering executive Chris Carradine described a place visitors could explore, with its story revealed in pieces. [The preopening article](https://disneydollars.net/index_html_files/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol23.3_1988_Summer.pdf#page=18) proposed a district that different ages could use, under different rules.

![Pleasure Island at dusk, with illuminated club signage reflected in the water and a footbridge in the foreground.](oip-image:essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/pleasure-island-1989)

*Pleasure Island at dusk, pictured in Disney's [1989 annual report, p. 10](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=14). Photograph: The Walt Disney Company.*

In [fall 1989](https://archive.org/download/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol24.4_1989.Fall/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol24.4_1989_Fall.pdf#page=40), Disney advertised shopping and dining access without an island admission charge. One evening charge covered entry to any or all of the clubs. Someone could come for dinner without making a night of the clubs.

By [summer 1990](https://archive.org/download/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol25.3_1990.Summer/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol25.3_1990_Summer.pdf#page=21), a $9.95 admission after 7 p.m. covered the clubs and a nightly New Year's celebration. The publicity emphasized an adult evening, while allowing younger children with a parent who understood that orientation. The [skating venue was now a beach-themed music club, and Videopolis was now Cage](https://archive.org/download/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol25.3_1990.Summer/DisneyNewsMagazine_Vol25.3_1990_Summer.pdf#page=22).

The new admission brought the clubs and street celebration together. Disney was adjusting how the night worked after guests began using the complex.

## Swan and Dolphin: New Hotels and Outside Investors

The Swan and Dolphin brought outside investors into the hotel expansion. In June 1988, writer Pilar Viladas described the proposed Swan and Dolphin in [Progressive Architecture](https://www.usmodernist.org/PA/PA-1988-06.pdf#page=106). The development and financing team included insurer Metropolitan Life Insurance, property and construction firm Tishman Realty & Construction, and Aoki, a [Japanese](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/fordirinv/fordirinv_comm_1988.pdf) [construction company](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1990-pt1/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1990-pt1-7-2.pdf#page=4). Tishman served as managing partner. The venture leased the land from Disney.

Disney Development, led by Peter Rummell, handled master planning and brought Michael Graves into the project. Graves's office designed the hotels, while Alan Lapidus's office supplied design development and working drawings. The article reported a $375 million project budget at that stage.

![A 1988 architectural model shows the proposed Dolphin hotel in the foreground and Swan behind it, facing each other across a lagoon.](oip-image:essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/swan-dolphin-model-1988)

*A model of Michael Graves's proposed Dolphin and Swan hotels, 1988. Photograph: William Taylor, [Progressive Architecture, June 1988, p. 105](https://www.usmodernist.org/PA/PA-1988-06.pdf#page=107).*

The model made the intended effect plain. Large sculptural figures would mark the hotels from a distance, above buildings facing one another across the water.

Disney's [1990 report](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633190.pdf#page=12) recorded the 758-room Swan opening in January and the 1,509-room Dolphin's premiere in June. Westin operated Swan, and Sheraton operated Dolphin.

More rooms followed nearby. The same report described Yacht and Beach Club opening in the fall, with 1,214 rooms and 73,000 square feet of meeting space. Robert Stern's designs drew on [New England seaside resorts](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633190.pdf#page=14). Port Orleans added a further 1,008 rooms when it [opened in 1991](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633189.pdf#page=17).

A convention needed places to assemble as well as places to sleep. The parks and evening venues gave that trip possibilities beyond the meeting schedule.

## How Disney Financed Its Florida Expansion

Disney's building program reached beyond Florida. For 1989, it reported operating cash flow of $1,275.6 million and investment in theme parks, resorts, and other property of $749.6 million. That [annual investment](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=46) covered Florida additions and work in California. It was a company-wide spending figure for one year, separate from the studio park's total project cost.

In April 1988, Disney received approximately $723 million by turning a substantial portion of future royalties from certain Tokyo Disneyland operations into present cash. The [financial note](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1988/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281988%29.pdf#page=45) covered royalties through April 2008 and said the arrangement reduced exposure to yen-dollar exchange fluctuations. Oriental Land Company remained Tokyo Disneyland's owner and operator.

Future receipts supplied cash Disney could use sooner. The report does not identify how much of that cash went into the Florida expansion.

Disney also borrowed. The [1989 financial discussion](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1989/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281989%29.pdf#page=44) linked increased debt to Euro Disneyland investment and advances and the acquisition of KCAL-TV. The report does not identify a separate construction loan for each Florida attraction.

Disney and the outside hotel businesses expanded what they could offer in Florida. They also had more buildings to operate and rooms to fill.

## Disney's Hotel Expansion Meets the 1991 Recession

The larger resort then met a less accommodating travel market. In its [1991 financial discussion](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633189.pdf#page=50), Disney attributed weaker park attendance to recession and softer domestic travel and tourism. Across Disney's resorts, the total number of occupied rooms rose only slightly from 1990, while added capacity lowered occupancy rates. Revenue from the additional occupied rooms did not cover all the extra costs of that capacity.

The company added Caribbean Beach to meet demand for moderately priced rooms. In 1991, Disney's [shareholder letter](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633189.pdf#page=5) reported 94 percent occupancy at its moderately priced hotels. That result applied to the category, alongside lower occupancy across the larger resort inventory.

The company's theme-park and resort results also reflected attendance and development costs. They do not establish what each hotel would earn over its life. A completed hotel carried expenses while waiting for someone to reserve the room.

Disney had given the Florida holiday more places to go. The bill might mean one less night, or the new park might be reason enough for one more. Beyond the day's last attraction, a hotel window could be lit and a waterfront band could be starting another set.

Tomorrow would have its own ticket.

For tonight, there was somewhere to be ~ *Disney.*
