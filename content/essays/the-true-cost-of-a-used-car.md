Imagine walking onto a used-car lot. A sedan catches your eye. You check the mileage, take it for a test drive, and listen for a rattle. Back at the salesman's desk, you offer less than the price on the windshield. He counters. You start working toward a number you can both accept.

Before you arrived, the dealership had to make its own decision about buying that car. What could it pay? What would it need to spend before selling it? How long might the car sit on the lot?

The purchase price is only the beginning. Transport, repairs, and time on the lot all affect what the dealer keeps. Before buying, the dealer has to judge whether the car is likely to sell for enough to cover those costs and leave a profit.

## Where dealers find their cars

Dealerships commonly source used vehicles from wholesale auctions, customer trade-ins, and private sellers. AutoNation's [2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/350698/000162828026007800/an-20251231.htm) lists customer trade-ins, direct purchases and auctions among its sources of used cars. Each route has its own costs and [risks](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/what-is-risk-a-four-part-framework/).

![Three roads connect an auction, a customer trade-in, and a private seller to one dealership. Auction bids can rise and inspection may be limited. Trade-ins are linked to another sale and require value and condition checks. Private deals require negotiation, inspection, and paperwork.](oip-image:essays/the-true-cost-of-a-used-car/section-1)

*Three common routes to a dealer's lot, each with its own work, costs, and risks.*

Auctions give dealers access to a range of vehicles in a short time. Competition can push bids upward, while limited opportunities to inspect a car make its condition harder to judge.

Taking a customer's old car may help the dealer sell another one. The dealer has to estimate what the old car will sell for, subtract the credit given to the customer, and allow for the cost of getting it ready.

Buying from a private seller requires time for negotiations, vehicle inspections, and checks on ownership and paperwork. A lower asking price may come with more work before the car is ready to sell.

## What might it sell for?

To estimate resale value, a dealer considers mileage, age, service history, accident records, mechanical condition, and local demand. Prices listed for similar cars help, but an advertised price is not necessarily the price a buyer will pay.

## The costs after the purchase

The purchase can bring transportation and administrative expenses. Then comes *reconditioning*: the repairs, cleaning, replacement parts, and inspections needed to prepare the car for sale. CarMax's [report for fiscal 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1170010/000117001026000021/kmx-20260228.htm) counts the purchase price, reconditioning and transport among the costs of the vehicles it sells. Underestimating that work reduces the expected profit.

A car ties up money while it waits for a buyer. The dealer may have to pay for storage and insurance. Dealers can use *floor plan loans* to finance cars held for sale. The [Office of the Comptroller of the Currency](https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/floor-plan-lending/pub-ch-floor-plan.pdf#page=9) says interest on these loans is usually based on the average daily balance owed. If that debt stays outstanding, waiting for a buyer can add to the interest bill. Until it sells, the money tied up in the car cannot pay for another car or other expenses.

Demand can weaken while a car sits, reducing its resale value. Interest rates can also affect buyers' budgets and the dealer's financing costs.

## What digital tools can tell a dealer

Online listings, vehicle-history services, valuation tools, and digital marketplaces help dealers compare vehicles and research prices across locations. Incomplete listings and regional differences in demand limit what those comparisons can show. Inspections and documentation checks remain essential.

Some dealers also use [Facebook ads](https://vehicquire.com/facebook-ads-for-car-dealers/) to reach owners considering a sale or trade-in. An inquiry is only a potential purchase. Dealers need to account for lead quality, staff time, negotiation costs, and how many inquiries produce suitable vehicles.

## Deciding how much to pay

A dealer's purchase limit rests on estimates of what the car might sell for and the costs it expects to incur. Raising that limit just to beat another bidder can use up the expected profit before the car reaches the lot.

![A ledger starts with expected selling price and subtracts estimated transport, preparation and administration, estimated holding costs, and target profit to reach an estimated purchase limit. Holding costs include storage, insurance, and any borrowing costs. Costs, selling time, and resale price can change.](oip-image:essays/the-true-cost-of-a-used-car/section-2)

*Work back from an expected selling price to estimate a buying limit. Holding costs here mean storage, insurance, and any borrowing costs.*

After a sale, the dealer can compare the actual preparation costs, selling price, and time on the lot with the earlier estimates. Those records help show what needs to change before the next purchase.

## Back at the desk

You lower your offer again. The salesman wants to make the sale, but he has to think about a few different things at once ~

*I would like a little more for it. But how much would we actually keep? We bought the car, got it here, and paid to get it ready. The gap between our purchase price and your offer is not all profit.*

*Those costs are already behind us. Now I have your offer in front of me. If I turn it down, how long might we wait for another buyer? The car keeps money tied up on the lot. Demand could slip. The next offer might be even lower...*

*Let me try a lower counteroffer. A higher price would help, but waiting for it could leave us with less.*

So he grits his teeth and starts negotiating.

"Let's make a deal. I'd love to get you into these wheels and out of here today! But I've got to put food on the table at home, and at that price, I'd be paying you to take the car..."
