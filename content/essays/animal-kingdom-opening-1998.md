*The first installment followed [Walt's Florida plans and Roy's work bringing the resort to opening](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/). The next two traced [EPCOT's change from a proposed city into a park](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/) and [the studios, nightlife, and hotels built around them](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/). The fourth begins with a sunrise.*

Animals gather beneath an African sky. At Pride Rock, Rafiki lifts a small lion into view. The opening of Disney's [1994 animated film, *The Lion King*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GibiNy4d4gc), makes the arrival of one cub an occasion for an entire landscape. "Circle of Life" gives the gathering its voice.

Four years later, the song accompanied another introduction. At Animal Kingdom's dedication on April 21, 1998, singer Lebo M performed it while costumed performers carried huge animal sculptures. Disney chief Michael Eisner dedicated the park with Roy E. Disney, Walt's nephew, beside him. Disney recorded the scene in its [release that day](https://allears.net/animal-kingdom/animal-kingdom-opening-day-april-22-1998/).

Beyond the ceremony stood a park that needed roads, buildings, water, food, and people who knew how to care for its animals. It also needed guests. Disney was adding a fourth major park to its Florida resort while building more hotels and attractions around it.

The expansion stretched back to the start of the 1990s. Families also gained a new way to pay for future visits.

## Disney World's 1990s Expansion: More Ways to Stay

The [hotel building of the late 1980s](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/disney-world-expansion-studios-pleasure-island-hotels/) gave Disney more rooms to sell. By 1991, the company was selling future stays as well as rooms for the next trip.

Disney Vacation Club began that year. Members paid up front and owed dues each year for a *real-estate interest* ~ an ownership interest in vacation property. They used points to book stays, with choices based on the season, length of the trip, and type of room or home. An exchange program offered trips beyond Disney too. The [1991 annual report](https://archive.org/details/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1991/mode/2up) explained the purchase and described the first homes being readied for guests.

A family had to weigh that commitment against booking one trip at a time.

Disney added more hotels too. Wilderness Lodge and All-Star Sports opened in 1994. One offered a grand lodge setting. The other added 1,920 rooms in what Disney's [annual report](https://archive.org/details/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1994/mode/2up) called its economy-priced category.

More rooms did not guarantee an easy season. Disney reported fewer visits to its theme parks in 1994 because tourism from abroad fell. More occupied hotel rooms and higher guest spending helped make up the loss. Across the parks-and-resorts division, which extended beyond Florida, [revenue barely changed and operating income fell eight percent](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1994/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281994%29.pdf#page=49).

## A Fourth Park Joins the Building Program

The existing parks continued to change. Tower of Terror at Disney-MGM Studios, Innoventions at EPCOT, and a new Tomorrowland at Magic Kingdom were among the [new attractions in Disney's 1994 report](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1994/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281994%29.pdf#page=36).

![Crane and construction scaffolding rise above the unfinished mountain at Blizzard Beach.](oip-image:essays/animal-kingdom-opening-1998/section-1)

*Blizzard Beach under construction in Disney's [1994 annual report](https://archive.org/download/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1994/Walt%20Disney%20Company%20%28DIS%29%20Annual%20Report%20%281994%29.pdf#page=33). A mountain and its water attractions were taking shape in Florida. © The Walt Disney Company.*

Blizzard Beach opened in 1995, carrying the unlikely story of a ski resort into a water park. It came with [chairlifts and a snow-covered setting](https://archive.org/details/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1995/mode/2up). Florida weather supplied a fairly good reason for the snow to melt.

Animal Kingdom entered the same building program. [Ron Hall reported in 1998](https://www.landscapemanagement.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/1998nov12l.pdf#page=2) that planning began in 1990. The first earthmoving followed in August 1995.

Disney's [1995 financial filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29082/0000898430-95-002664.txt) recorded $896.5 million in company-wide property spending. The report named the new park alongside BoardWalk, Blizzard Beach, cruise ships, and Celebration. It did not break out that year's spending on Animal Kingdom.

Disney's [1997 preview](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/decmed/46860/iaDISNEYa1997ieng.pdf#page=22) described a planned 500-acre park where guests could take a safari and a dinosaur ride in the same day.

Around the construction site, the rest of the resort kept opening for business.

BoardWalk opened in 1996 with a hotel, Vacation Club rooms, and places to eat and spend the evening. In his [letter to shareholders that year](https://archive.org/details/walt-disney-company-dis-annual-report-1996-c), Eisner set out more plans for the resort. Hotels and convention space appeared alongside sports, Animal Kingdom, and cruises.

Coronado Springs opened in August 1997. Wide World of Sports and Downtown Disney West Side also opened that year, as Disney's [1997 report](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/decmed/46860/iaDISNEYa1997ieng.pdf#page=28) recorded.

A business meeting or a sporting event could bring guests who might not otherwise plan a theme-park trip. Once there, they could use the same hotels and restaurants as families on holiday.

## How Disney Financed Its 1990s Expansion

In its opening coverage, *Travel Weekly* described Animal Kingdom as an [approximately $800 million park](https://www.travelweekly.com/Destinations2001-2007/Animal-Kingdom-Welcomes-First-Visitors). Disney's accounts show how it raised money while building the park and its other projects.

In 1997, outside investors bought ownership interests in two Disney *real-estate investment trusts*, which hold investments in property. The [annual report](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/decmed/46860/iaDISNEYa1997ieng.pdf#page=64) does not name the investors or link their money to Animal Kingdom.

Disney also brought in cash from its businesses and borrowed as a company. In fiscal 1998, *operating cash flow* ~ cash generated after day-to-day business payments ~ totaled $5.115 billion. New borrowing brought in $1.830 billion, with debt repayments listed separately. The parks-and-resorts division spent $1.693 billion on *capital projects*, such as buildings and equipment. These [company and division totals](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1001039/0000898430-98-004473.txt) cover more than the new Florida park.

Disney was paying for several projects at once. The reports do not identify a separate set of investors or loans that paid for Animal Kingdom.

## Building Animal Kingdom's Living Landscape

Designer Joe Rohde explained a design choice to the park's opening team. In a [transcript of his 1998 talk, published in edited form in 2019](https://allears.net/walt-disney-world-chronicles-joe-rohde-and-disneys-animal-kingdom-in-1988/), he described bright colors and animal ornament around Safari Village. Across its bridges, the surrounding lands would look weathered and worn. He wanted each return through the village to prepare guests for another adventure.

At the park's center, the Tree of Life required steel before it could have branches.

According to the [company's 1997 account](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/decmed/46860/iaDISNEYa1997ieng.pdf#page=3), the Tree of Life used 175 tons of steel in its branches. Thirteen sculptors spent eighteen months working on its animal forms.

![The Tree of Life stands against a pink and gold sky, reflected in water.](oip-image:essays/animal-kingdom-opening-1998/section-3)

*The Tree of Life in Disney's [1997 annual report](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/decmed/46860/iaDISNEYa1997ieng.pdf#page=23), before the park opened. © The Walt Disney Company.*

The great tree could be built to a chosen shape. The plants around it needed to grow.

Reporting for *Landscape Management* in November 1998, Ron Hall described landscape architect Paul Comstock riding the safari route on a motorcycle, marking future planting beds with spray paint. Craftsmen pressed tire marks and vegetation into wet concrete to age the road. What looked like tree stumps hid watering stations. Hall's [visit and staff interviews](https://www.landscapemanagement.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/1998nov12l.pdf) showed the work needed to make a new landscape look long settled.

The animals could change the plans too. Hall described an elephant pulling down a palm about twenty feet tall. Dennis Higbie, the curator in charge of the plants, considered leaving that spot without a new tree. "Maybe we won't put the palm tree back there," he said. The same [1998 account](https://www.landscapemanagement.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/1998nov12l.pdf#page=4) described crews tending the landscape at night, after the large animals moved into their buildings for feeding and bedding down.

A planting plan could guide the work. It could not settle every question once an elephant arrived.

At the [dedication on April 21](https://allears.net/animal-kingdom/animal-kingdom-opening-day-april-22-1998/), Roy E. Disney compared the park with a film. "Once a movie is completed, it's done forever," he said, before describing the park as something that would continue to change.

## Animal Kingdom's 1998 Opening and First Season

The park opened to the public the day after its dedication. On April 22, Earth Day, the guests came in. Disney's [opening release](https://allears.net/animal-kingdom/animal-kingdom-opening-day-april-22-1998/) identified Brenda Herr, Damon Chepren, and their son Devon as the first guest family. They slept in their car the night before to secure that place.

In July 1998, Disney's [ticket page](https://web.archive.org/web/19980704024958/http://www2.disney.com:80/DisneyWorld/ThemeParks/Par45.html) listed one-day, one-park admission at $42 for adults and $34 for children ages three through nine, plus tax.

Inside, guests could choose how to spend the day. The opening-year [Adventurer's Guide](https://mousemuseum.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/dak98_2.jpg) directed visitors to Kilimanjaro Safaris, the dinosaur journey called Countdown to Extinction, and the insect show *It's Tough to Be a Bug!* Conservation Station introduced animal care. In Camp Minnie-Mickey, *Festival of the Lion King* brought the film's characters and music into a live show.

![The September 1998 Animal Kingdom guide maps Safari Village, Africa, DinoLand U.S.A., Camp Minnie-Mickey, and the first Asia attraction.](oip-image:essays/animal-kingdom-opening-1998/section-4)

*Animal Kingdom's [September 14-20, 1998 guide](https://mousemuseum.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/dak98_2.jpg) shows the opening-year park and advertises more of Asia for 1999. The September guide already listed Flights of Wonder in Asia while advertising additional Asia attractions for early 1999. Opening the park did not finish the plan. As with [EPCOT's first years](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/), a visitor could enjoy the completed attractions while others remained ahead.

## What Animal Kingdom Added to Disney World

Disney credited Animal Kingdom with helping Walt Disney World reach record attendance in fiscal 1998. Its [year-end filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1001039/0000898430-98-004473.txt) also recorded the costs of opening and running the park and the new cruise business. Across the parks-and-resorts division, revenue rose ten percent and operating income thirteen percent.

Disney was also offering a way to take the holiday beyond Florida. At the opening luncheon, Disney executive Randy Garfield described the combination of a resort visit and a cruise, according to [*Travel Weekly's* April 23 report](https://www.travelweekly.com/Destinations2001-2007/Animal-Kingdom-Welcomes-First-Visitors). More of Asia was to come, and the vacation could continue at sea.

At Animal Kingdom, meanwhile, the work would continue after the visitors left. There were animals to feed and a landscape to tend before the next morning. The giant tree could hold its pose. The living things around it would have their own ideas. When the gates opened again, another family would arrive with a map and a day to spend. Somewhere beyond the bend, something would move ~ and for a moment, the whole day would stand still.
