*Brands, Business, and Consumer Choice · Disney World Development, Part Two: The EPCOT Project*

*[The Florida Project, the first installment in this series](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/), follows Walt's plans and Roy's role in completing the vacation resort that opened on October 1, 1971. Those plans also called for a working community. This installment traces how EPCOT evolved into the park that opened in 1982.*

## Walt Disney's EPCOT City Plan and the PeopleMover

In November 1973, EPCOT's planners were considering the trip to work. Don Edgren's record of Bob Gurr's comments pitted the frustrations of driving and finding time for exercise against the possibility of living close enough to walk. Other entries concerned schools, home maintenance, mixed incomes and how long residents might remain in the community. The [surviving notes](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_97a7d851705845bbb7faf36149825ee5.pdf#page=2) follow people through the ordinary arrangements of a working week.

Edgren's memorandum, dated November 29, recorded the previous day's EPCOT meeting. On December 18, Marty Sklar circulated further material and proposed more investigation and assignments. Their work was exploratory, with the practical terms of living there open for discussion.

The hotels, boats and monorails were already receiving vacationers. The company was operating the Florida park while working out the community proposed beyond it.

EPCOT stands for "Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow." [Walt's proposed working city](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/#the-working-city-walt-proposed) joined employment and housing to a coordinated transportation system. The company's [1967 Project Florida booklet, published after his death](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_95503446f8c346fba334b9b8f4f1344c.pdf#page=13), arranged apartments, a green belt and lower-density homes around a central commercial district, with workplaces in an industrial area.

The WEDway PeopleMover would provide local transportation in small cars following a fixed route. A worker could leave home, follow a pedestrian path to a neighborhood station and ride into downtown. In the [transportation lobby beneath EPCOT's central hotel](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_95503446f8c346fba334b9b8f4f1344c.pdf#page=15), the worker would transfer to a monorail for the industrial complex. The [booklet illustrated that daily commute](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_95503446f8c346fba334b9b8f4f1344c.pdf#page=16). The PeopleMover would connect neighborhoods to the center; the monorail would carry passengers farther across the development. Housing, jobs and transportation were being planned together.

International commerce was considered in the plan as well. The central shopping district would contain foreign restaurants, imported goods, and streets representing other countries. That idea would appear again in the World Showcase, although the originally proposed city center and the actual park would follow different designs.

## Lake Buena Vista and Disney's Housing Plans

The land outside [the resort that opened in 1971](https://outsideinprint.org/essays/walt-disney-world-opening-1971/#what-opened-in-1971) supported several kinds of development. Magic Kingdom was one part of Walt Disney World. Lake Buena Vista was a distinct real-estate project within the larger property, and EPCOT remained a longer-term, aspirational proposal.

By 1972, Disney was operating without either founder. Its annual report carried a [tribute to Walt and Roy](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1972/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201972.pdf#page=4). [Donn Tatum chaired the board and Card Walker was president](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1972/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201972.pdf#page=38). They had big shoes to fill ~ an already operating resort to manage and further Florida proposals to consider.

Disney's [1972 annual report](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1972/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201972.pdf#page=13) recorded eighty completed and leased townhouses, with another fifty-three under construction. Disney described the community as primarily second homes and corporate executive retreats. A visiting executive or vacationing household would make different demands on the property from a family living there full-time.

Architect Peter Blake made the distinction while reporting on the property for the [June 1972 Architectural Forum](https://usmodernist.org/AF/AF-1972-06.pdf#page=32). He described the initial townhouse construction while treating the larger EPCOT city as tentative. The accompanying site plan separately identified Buena Vista, EPCOT and the vacation facilities. The smaller community taking shape was distinct from the larger city being proposed.

Other residential proposals encountered obstacles. In 1974, Disney reported that the Grant Corporation had found one site unsuitable for its intended retirement development. Financing for other proposed housing was slowed by economic conditions affecting residential construction. Disney's [1974 report](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1974/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201974.pdf#page=21) documents setbacks to these Lake Buena Vista housing proposals. However, it doesn't explain the broader shift from Walt's proposed working city to the EPCOT Center that eventually opened.

That year's report described a broader EPCOT of research and activity centers. Experimental living environments appeared alongside work on energy, education and other subjects. World Showcase, a permanent international cultural and commercial exhibition, was proposed as the first major step. Its [semicircular buildings and central courtyard](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1974/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201974.pdf#page=24) had yet to become the national pavilions around the lagoon familiar to later visitors.

Disney was developing a resort while considering several different ways to expand it. The name "EPCOT" covered an expanding field of experiments and attractions, with their sequence and financing being worked out.

## EPCOT's Shift From Residents to Visitors

In 1975, company president E. Cardon Walker explained a change in purpose. A conventional community serving permanent residents, he argued, could become obsolete by the time it was completed. His [annual-report statement](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_26d6566e2c6645fd92b3aec0921e1c2a.pdf#page=3) proposed reaching millions with new ideas. It moved beyond serving the "day-to-day needs of a limited number of permanent residents."

The proposed arrangement had several parts ~ an EPCOT Institute to organize participation, research satellites to test ideas, and a Future World Theme Center to explain them to visitors. The center was proposed as [nonprofit and free to Walt Disney World guests](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_26d6566e2c6645fd92b3aec0921e1c2a.pdf#page=7). World Showcase would be a separately ticketed international exposition.

*The 1975 World Showcase model used two semicircular buildings for national displays. Disney's accompanying caption described a monorail link to the separate EPCOT Theme Center. [Model photograph](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_7fba7367acb9489d9e9698ddfb998669.pdf#page=6) and [original caption](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_7fba7367acb9489d9e9698ddfb998669.pdf#page=7). © Walt Disney Productions.*

Disney executives anticipated longer visits and further business for the resort. Public demonstration and the vacation business could support each other quite nicely.

The "PeopleMover" is a good example of how the company was positioning itself. By 1975, visitors could ride WEDway through Magic Kingdom's Tomorrowland. Disney's report described the [linear-motor-propelled installation](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_26d6566e2c6645fd92b3aec0921e1c2a.pdf#page=16) and included transportation development among its EPCOT research interests. The citywide residential network remained a proposal, while a related system was already carrying guests through the park.

## Future World and World Showcase Become One Park

By 1976, Walker was reconsidering how to bring the two visitor attractions together. He said the response from industry, government and academic circles had led him to rethink opening World Showcase on its own. He asked WED Enterprises, Disney's design organization, to look at developing it alongside other parts of EPCOT.

The [1976 account](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_8f31df27284145f6911ec907fd3020c3.pdf#page=3) described a single center with a shared main entrance. Guests would buy tickets and visit both World Showcase and Future World. World Showcase would lead them toward Future World, much as the entrance street led into Disney's existing parks. Walker also saw a business advantage ~ combining the attractions could help bring in more sponsors.

[Sklar, John Hench and John DeCuir](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_8f31df27284145f6911ec907fd3020c3.pdf#page=4) worked on the designs. National displays and corporate exhibits would share a destination, giving Disney a larger attraction to offer prospective participants.

The plans kept changing. By the [1977 Master Plan 5](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_b3d6622110af4a9e99573c2e6ae9d724.pdf#page=10), Spaceship Earth stood at the entrance to Future World, with its themed pavilions and CommuniCore exhibition area. World Showcase's national pavilions surrounded a broad lagoon. The layout was beginning to resemble the park visitors would recognize, with further changes ahead of it.

World Showcase would offer culture, food and commerce, while Future World would explore technology and practical concerns. Together, they gave a vacationing family more reasons to spend a day beyond Magic Kingdom.

## Disney Sets EPCOT's 1982 Opening Date

On October 2, 1978, Walker addressed the International Chamber of Commerce's World Congress at Walt Disney World. He announced that Disney would proceed with EPCOT Center, at an [estimated initial cost of about $500 million](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_b0d7a4a5c41a4b3c9dc0ddf3daa7b79a.pdf#page=4). The [published announcement](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_b0d7a4a5c41a4b3c9dc0ddf3daa7b79a.pdf#page=3) also set an opening date ~ October 1, 1982. Disney now had a deadline to work toward.

General Motors, Exxon, Kraft and AT&T were named at the contract or letter-of-intent stage. Walker also reported prospective participation from business or government interests in ten countries. The company was assembling its participants, and the list would change before opening.

The report proposed [one admission covering all pavilions and adventures](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_b0d7a4a5c41a4b3c9dc0ddf3daa7b79a.pdf#page=9). Guests would buy admission to the whole experience, and Disney would have to give them enough to make the visit worthwhile.

A year later, the price being discussed had changed. At the ceremonial groundbreaking on October 1, 1979, the [annual report described an $800 million project](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_126f4687619d4b56b987ea8e590afd48.pdf#page=4). Governors past and present joined Walker for the occasion. The public ceremony was one part of the process, with design, site work and financing each following its own schedule.

Practical experiments also continued on the property. A 1976 WED meeting on water hyacinths led to a wastewater-treatment pilot approved for funding in 1978 and initially operating in 1979. In the [EPA seminar proceedings](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CZIC-td523-a68-1979/pdf/CZIC-td523-a68-1979.pdf#page=254), Reedy Creek engineer Andrew Kruzic described contributions from NASA, EPA, Disney's organizations and other participants, along with startup problems and unresolved questions. That research support concerned the pilot, separately from financing the new park.

## How Disney Financed EPCOT Center

Disney also had to work out how to pay for the development. As Roy's earlier work had shown, announcing a price and finding the money were two different jobs. The company drew on its own resources, outside financing and agreements with participants.

In October 1979, Disney entered a five-year revolving credit agreement for $200 million with Bank of America. Walker reported the arrangement in his [shareholder letter](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_126f4687619d4b56b987ea8e590afd48.pdf#page=3). The agreement made up to $200 million available for the company to borrow.

The [1981 accounts](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1981/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201981.pdf#page=34) recorded a $100 million Eurodollar note issue in September, carrying 15.75 percent interest. Both arrangements supported Disney's wider investment program. The reports don't give an EPCOT-only total for either one.

Participants also paid money in advance. Disney's [1982 financial review](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_84f3d823ccda4551a791c34898378a3d.pdf#page=36) described how outside financing and participant advances, along with cash and investments accumulated in earlier years, helped meet the company's funding needs.

Some of those business relationships would be easy for visitors to spot. The opening [participant list](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=79) paired Bell System with Spaceship Earth, Exxon with the Universe of Energy, General Motors with World of Motion, Kraft with The Land and Kodak with Imagination.

![Visitors approach The Land pavilion past an entrance sign bearing Kraft's name.](oip-image:essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/the-land-1982)

For guests, some of those business arrangements became names attached to the buildings and experiences they came to see.

## EPCOT Center Construction: Costs and Deadlines

By February 1981, the project team was tracking schedules and costs line by line. A WED status report dated February 20, with a January 24 financial cutoff, identified Jim Nagy as EPCOT project manager and Hal McIntyre as business administrator.

The [internal account](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_9d04157cc61e412f894b47078e30e769.pdf#page=4) put the overall project about six weeks behind its then-current schedule. Manpower was above plan, while spending was below projections. The estimated final cost stood at about $803.7 million against the report's internal budget of $781.5 million. That was the team's estimate at that point in the work.

Changes to the plans also changed the estimate. Removing Morocco from the opening-day program brought it down, while revisions to Imagination added cost. The team had to keep updating the budget as decisions about the park were made.

The buildings, shows and systems for moving people through them all had to work together. The February report left the team with time to make up before October 1982.

Later that year, the PeopleMover found work beyond Disney's parks. The company's [1981 report](https://archive.org/download/wdp-annual-report-1981/WDP%20Annual%20Report%201981.pdf#page=21) described an operating WEDway system linking terminals, a hotel and parking at Houston's airport. The residential city remained a proposal, but the transportation work was already carrying people through an airport as well as a theme park.

## EPCOT's Opening Day: October 1, 1982

Guests were getting a look at EPCOT in late September 1982. The September 30 issue of [Disneyland Line](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_fd716642691c4ab18b70963ebc323cc5.pdf#page=3) described the preceding three days as previews and looked ahead to the following day's opening celebration. [Disney's financial accounts use September 28](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_84f3d823ccda4551a791c34898378a3d.pdf#page=36), while October 1 was the official opening date.

On October 1, WED president Carl Bongirno acknowledged the opening in a [letter to the team](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_4cea1b9c4faf49208d52d401c9995a16.pdf#page=2) that also recognized the work ahead. Walker's main dedication remarks followed on [October 24](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_84f3d823ccda4551a791c34898378a3d.pdf#page=5).

![Guests walk beside flower beds toward Spaceship Earth at EPCOT Center.](oip-image:essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/spaceship-earth-1982)

*Guests approach Spaceship Earth, circa 1982. Florida Division of Tourism photograph, [State Archives of Florida, COM03857](https://www.floridamemory.com/items/show/94073). Public domain.*

For a visiting family, there was a simpler set of numbers to consider. The [opening information sheet](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=81) listed adult one-day admission at $15 and daily parking at $1. Lodging, meals and purchases would add to a family's vacation bill.

Inside, [Spaceship Earth carried visitors through a story of communication](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=12). [The Land offered a journey through agricultural environments](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=23). Across World Showcase stood [Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Japan, the United States, Italy, Germany, China and Mexico](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=79). A family could spend part of the day exploring technology and another part walking through the national displays.

![Guests walk among the United Kingdom pavilion's streets and buildings.](oip-image:essays/epcot-center-opening-1982/united-kingdom-1982)

*The United Kingdom pavilion, circa 1982. Florida Division of Tourism photograph, [State Archives of Florida, COM03926](https://www.floridamemory.com/items/show/94085). Public domain.*

More was coming. [Horizons was announced for 1983](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=19). Kodak's [Imagination fact sheet](https://wdwnt-buzzy.imgix.net/2020/04/JourneyIntoImaginationPressKit1982_small_Page_2.jpg) scheduled the pavilion's principal ride for a later opening, separately from its other experiences. Being included in the opening publicity didn't mean every part was ready on October 1.

Disney subsequently reported [a cost of $913 million for facilities completed and operational at opening](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_84f3d823ccda4551a791c34898378a3d.pdf#page=36). It estimated a further $240 million for additional attractions and exhibits. Those figures covered different stages and scopes of work from the earlier announcements and the internal construction estimate.

Walker and company president Ron Miller called EPCOT Center ["our interpretation of Walt's dream"](https://www.disneydocs.net/_files/ugd/5db718_f57a8b7b47ec41d3b4cff123301e0195.pdf#page=10). Visitors could see what they had carried forward ~ technology, international exchange and public demonstration, now with buildings to enter and experiences to try. The proposed residential city remained unbuilt.

EPCOT gave families another day to plan, and Disney another reason to build. An extra day in Florida could mean another hotel night, another meal and another chance to make the trip worth repeating. Within a few years, [work would be under way on the Disney-MGM studio park, a new resort hotel and a nighttime entertainment district](https://public-content.library.mcgill.ca/digitization/633194.pdf#page=10). The next decade would bring another round of plans, financing and decisions about how much of a holiday Disney could offer. Each new attraction or hotel would need a guest with a reason to walk through the door. The castle and the silver sphere would soon have company.
