Say you're ten words over a submission limit. You open a counter, paste the whole draft, and get a number. It takes a few seconds. The draft might include an unpublished interview, a client's plans, or a detail someone told you in confidence. To get the number, you may have handed over much more than the tool needed.

Before pasting, I'd ask three questions. Where does the text go? Who can receive it (and why)? What copies might remain when I'm done?

## Where does the text go?

A browser can count words on your device. Other features, such as an AI rewrite, may send the same text to a server. A word counter and an AI rewriter can look alike on the same site. [WordCount's privacy page](https://wordcount.com/privacy), for example, says its counting tools run in the browser while its AI writing tools send text to a model for processing. Look for what the site says about the feature you're actually using.

![Same Site, Two Paths. At WordCount, the word counter works in the browser. The AI rewriter sends text to an AI model for processing.](oip-image:essays/ten-words-over/section-1)

*WordCount says it sends text to an AI model for rewriting and discards the text after processing. Outside In Print graphic.*

I own and operate a [character counter, now hosted on KitGlade](https://kitglade.com/resources/text-utilities/character-counter/), that also counts words. When I tested the counter in Chrome on November 11, 2026, it counted eight words in my made-up sample. I saw no new request in the Network panel while the count ran.

If you want to try this yourself, open [Chrome's Network panel](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/network/reference) before entering anything. Make up a sentence with a distinctive word. Watch for new requests when you paste it and click the button. If a request appears, select it. In **Headers**, check the request URL to see where it went. If you see a **Payload** tab, look there for your made-up word. A page can load a font or record a visit without sending your draft. A test tells you what happened in that session. Run it again if you switch features or change account settings.

![Try It With Made-Up Text. Open Chrome's Network panel, paste a made-up sentence, and run the tool. If a request appears, select it, check the URL in Headers, and look for your word in Payload if shown. Otherwise, no request was seen in this test.](oip-image:essays/ten-words-over/section-2)

*Use a made-up sentence to see what the tool sends during the test. Outside In Print graphic.*

## Who gets it, and what can they do with it?

If the text goes to a server, check the terms for the feature and account you're using. Find out whether another company processes your words and whether a person can review them. Then look for what the service says about using your text to improve or train a model. Even if it rules out training, check whether it keeps a copy and who can see it.

The [Authors Guild urges publishing professionals](https://authorsguild.org/news/use-of-ai-in-publishing-and-new-model-contract-clause/) to get an author's written permission before putting a manuscript into a consumer AI tool. If the material belongs to a client, a source, or another writer, check what they've agreed to before you share it with a service.

## What stays behind?

A tool can keep your words on your device and show them again after you reload the page. That can be convenient on your own computer and a problem on one you share. Check whether the tool saves a draft on your device or copies it to your account. If the service gets your text, check how long it keeps a copy and whether deleting the draft from the tool's history removes that copy. An empty text box tells you what's visible on screen.

![What Can Stay Behind? An empty field shows what is on screen. The browser may save a draft on this device, and the service may keep a copy.](oip-image:essays/ten-words-over/section-3)

*The box can be empty even if your browser or the service has a copy. Outside In Print graphic.*

## Back to the word count

For that ten-word problem, I'd start with the count in the editor I already use, or an offline editor if the draft needs to stay off a server. If an online tool does something I actually need, I can often give it a smaller excerpt I'm free to share or a fictional sample. For a sensitive draft, an unclear answer about where the words go is reason enough to keep the file where it is.
