On the morning of November 11, 1918, U.S. Army Sgt. Thomas P. Shinn&#8217;s unit was under fire in France. German machine guns fired from the woods ahead. Shells fell behind the men, and barbed wire blocked their way forward. At eleven o&#8217;clock, Shinn&#8217;s unit stopped firing. He saw German soldiers throw down their rifles and run toward the American line. [Shinn&#8217;s diary, transcribed by the State Archives of North Carolina](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2018/11/11/north-carolinians-respond-armistice-november-11-1918).

> They wanted to shake hands and talk with us but we made them go back.

An *armistice* is an agreement to stop fighting. The agreement that Germany signed with the Allied and associated powers gives the signing time as five in the morning, French time. It called for fighting on land and in the air to stop six hours later. That evening, Shinn wrote, German soldiers sent up flares from their trenches in celebration. He and the other Americans were &#8220;perfectly willing to rest and sleep.&#8221; [1918 armistice agreement](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv02/d1), [Shinn&#8217;s diary](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2018/11/11/north-carolinians-respond-armistice-november-11-1918).

In his message for the first anniversary, President Woodrow Wilson recalled more than four years of fighting by the European Allies. To help them, he wrote, the United States reorganized its industries, increased farm production, and raised an army. The armistice gave nations a chance to work out fairer relations in peace, Wilson said. [Wilson&#8217;s message, November 11, 1919, printed p. 1844](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/publications/cfc/cfc_19191115.pdf#page=28).

> Out of this victory there arose new possibilities of political freedom and economic concert. The war showed us the strength of great nations acting together for high purposes, and the victory of arms foretells the enduring conquests which can be made in peace when nations act justly and in furtherance of the common interests of men. To us in America the reflections of Armistice Day will be filled with solemn pride in the heroism of those who died in the country&#8217;s service, and with gratitude for the victory, both because of the thing from which it has freed us, and because of the opportunity it has given America to show her sympathy with peace and justice in the councils of nations.

*Children cheered and beat tin cans at Portland&#8217;s 1919 Armistice Day parade.*

Thousands crowded the sidewalks in Portland, Oregon, for a parade marking the first anniversary of the armistice. The *Oregon Daily Journal* described uniforms brought out after months in storage. Boy Scouts waved flags and boasted about older brothers who served overseas. Girls were &#8220;waving rattlers and beating tin cans.&#8221; A year earlier, the paper recalled, people had cheered even as they worried that a son, brother, husband, or friend might have died in the final weeks of the war. [*Oregon Daily Journal*, November 11, 1919](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn85042444/1919-11-11/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).

The American Legion post in Medford, Oregon, advertised a parade with veterans of the Civil War, the Spanish-American War, and the Great War. It asked former Allied service members to come in uniform and invited the public to a ball that evening with a ten-piece jazz orchestra. [American Legion notice in the *Medford Mail Tribune*, November 8, 1919](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1919-11-08/ed-1/seq-3.pdf).

President Warren G. Harding declared November 11, 1921, a holiday for the burial of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington. The unidentified soldier stood for the Americans who died in the war. Harding asked public and church bells to toll before noon. At noon Washington time, he asked the country to pause for two minutes of silent prayer. [Harding&#8217;s proclamation, November 4, 1921](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-42/pdf/STATUTE-42-Pg2255.pdf).

![Mourners bow beside a flag-draped casket in a low marble crypt on the east plaza of Arlington's Memorial Amphitheater.](oip-image:essays/what-armistice-day-meant/section-2)

*Illustration of the Unknown Soldier&#8217;s burial at Arlington on November 11, 1921.*

By 1926, twenty-seven states observed November 11 as a legal holiday, Congress reported. It asked the president to call for flags on government buildings and for ceremonies in schools, churches, and other places. People would give thanks for peace and express a wish for friendly relations with other nations. In 1938, Congress made Armistice Day an annual federal holiday. The law called November 11 &#8220;a day to be dedicated to the cause of world peace.&#8221; [1926 congressional resolution](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-44/pdf/STATUTE-44-Pg1982-3.pdf), [1938 law](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-52/pdf/STATUTE-52-Pg351-3.pdf).

Medford held its 1945 Armistice Day parade on Monday, November 12, because the eleventh fell on a Sunday. Members of all the city&#8217;s veterans&#8217; organizations took part, the *Medford Mail Tribune* reported. The parade started at eleven in the morning, the hour the guns stopped in France twenty-seven years earlier. [*Medford Mail Tribune*, November 12, 1945](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn97071090/1945-11-12/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/).

Medford&#8217;s Legion post had invited veterans of three wars to march in 1919, decades before Congress took up the question of whom the federal holiday should honor. World War II and the Korean War added millions of veterans. In 1954, the House Judiciary Committee described how Americans used Armistice Day to honor those who fought in World War I, especially those who died. The committee proposed using the same holiday to honor all American veterans. [House Report No. 1333, March 9, 1954](https://www.archives.gov/files/legislative/resources/education/process/mini-facsimiles.pdf#page=4).

On June 1, 1954, President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed a bill amending the 1938 law. November 11 remained a holiday dedicated to world peace. The amendment replaced &#8220;Armistice&#8221; with &#8220;Veterans&#8221; in the holiday&#8217;s name. [Public Law 83-380](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-68/pdf/STATUTE-68-Pg168.pdf).
