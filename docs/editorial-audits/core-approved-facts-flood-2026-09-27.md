## 1. Flash Flood Alley: Why Flash Floods Kill

File: `content/essays/flash-flood-alley-why-flash-floods-kill.md`

1. Before: `Rain doesn't soak in.`
   After: `Rain can fall faster than the ground can absorb it.`
2. Before: `Llano River, 2018: Crested at a record 39.9 feet, snapping the 85-year-old FM 2900 bridge in half.`
   After: `Llano River, 2018: Crested at 39.9 feet, snapping the 49-year-old RM 2900 bridge in half.`
3. Before: `Guadalupe River, 1987: A church van was swept away in Hunt, killing 10 teenagers.`
   After: `Guadalupe River, 1987: A church bus and van became stranded near Comfort; ten teenagers died after leaving the vehicles.`
4. Before: `These alerts follow a standard progression:`
   After: `These alert types do not require a fixed progression:`
5. Before: `Once these alerts are issued, local officials and automated systems have to move warnings into mass notification channels like CodeRED or Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEAs).`
   After: `NWS warnings tagged considerable or catastrophic trigger Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEAs) without waiting for local CodeRED approval. Local officials operate CodeRED as a separate notification channel.`
6. Before: `Rain gauges weren't functioning`
   After: `River-gauge coverage was missing upstream of Hunt on the South Fork`
7. Before: `The ground here is made of limestone, which doesn't absorb water.`
   After: `The ground here includes limestone, and heavy rain can exceed its ability to absorb water.`
8. Before: `It's a mix of delayed warnings, offline gauges, power outages, and poor communication. Flash Flood Warnings were issued starting at 1:14 a.m., but evacuation didn't begin until close to 4:45 a.m. By 5:15 a.m., it was too late.`
   After: `It's a mix of warning-to-action delays, gauge-coverage gaps, power outages, and poor communication. Flash Flood Warnings were issued starting at 1:14 a.m. The Texas Legislature's June 2026 report places the start of cabin evacuation around 3:00 a.m., after water had begun entering cabins.`
9. Before: `This is the highest alert the National Weather Service can issue.`
   After: `This is the highest-severity designation in a flash flood warning.`

Evidence:

- [NWS, Flood Related Hazards](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-hazards), the preserved source review of rainfall intensity, infiltration and runoff: limestone is not categorically impermeable. These two clauses no longer make that absolute assertion; no new runoff model is implied.
- [TxDOT, RM 2900 bridge replacement](https://www.txdot.gov/projects/projects-studies/austin/rm2900-kingsland-bridge-replacement.html), read directly: constructed in 1969 and washed away October 16, 2018; largest local flood since 1935, not an all-time record. The unchanged `39.9 feet` still requires its own gauge source.
- [NWS, July 17, 1987 event](https://www.weather.gov/ewx/wxevent-19870717), read directly and preserved in the prior source record: stranded bus and van near Comfort, with ten teenage fatalities after leaving the vehicles. This corrects event location and mechanism, not just the vehicle label.
- [NWS, WEA flash-flood changes](https://www.weather.gov/mkx/flashflood_updates_WEA), read directly: considerable/catastrophic warnings trigger WEA. [Commerce OIG, April 9, 2026](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-017-I-SECURED.pdf), printed pp. 5, 10–13 / PDF pp. 9, 14–17, read directly: separate federal warning dissemination and local contacts; absence of upstream South Fork river gauges, not proof that all rain gauges malfunctioned.
- [Texas Legislature, June 18, 2026 report](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/355/Report-on-the-Camp-Mystic-Flood-Disaster-of-July-4-2025.pdf?d=06182026#page=80), printed pp. 76–83 in the preserved primary-source inspection: around 3:00 a.m. cabin evacuation, following water entering cabins. Current web-reader searches failed; that failure is not treated as verification. The already documented primary passages and the essay's companion timeline supply the specific corrected sequence.

The main timeline's `~4:45 a.m.` statement is explicitly attributed to early public reporting and says efforts were underway, not that they first began then. It was preserved. The categorical FAQ start-time statement was corrected with a conspicuously dated retrospective source. The author's criticism of preparedness remains.

Version recommendation: **major**, because event timing, warning authority and historical facts materially change citation-relevant copy.

Remaining hold: legacy technical diagrams and their timing/authority claims; the precise power-outage interval; the presentation of 4:22 a.m. CodeRED as if it were the first relevant message; separate historical flood numbers; quotation provenance; and unsupported timing/certainty outside the corrected FAQ. ## 2. Who's Responsible for Building in the Floodplain?

File: `content/essays/whos-responsible-for-building-in-the-floodplain.md`

1. Before: `The federal government doesn't control zoning and development law.`
   After: `The federal government doesn't exercise general local zoning authority.`
2. Before: `The federal government does not have the authority to restrict construction on private and state-owned property.`
   After: `The federal government can restrict some construction on private and state-owned property through laws such as dredged-or-fill-material permit requirements.`
3. Before: `That being said, zoning law is determined by local governments, period.`
   After: `That being said, local governments generally administer zoning under state law.`
4. Before: `All FEMA can do is communicate the risk.`
   After: `FEMA also conditions flood-insurance eligibility on floodplain-management standards.`
5. Before: `By the time Camp Mystic began evacuating around 4:45 a.m., it was already too late for many.`
   After: `The Texas Legislature's June 2026 report places the start of cabin evacuation around 3:00 a.m., after water had begun entering cabins.`
6. Before list items: `The National Weather Service issues the alerts.` / `Local officials get the message to the people on the ground.`
   After: `The National Weather Service issues the alerts; considerable and catastrophic flash flood warnings trigger Wireless Emergency Alerts.` / `Local officials operate additional notification channels, such as CodeRED; they do not have to approve NWS Wireless Emergency Alerts.`
7. Before answer to “Can the Federal Government Force People to Move?”: `No.`
   After: `Not through FEMA's voluntary buyout program.`
8. Before: `It can't force local governments to evacuate a town, shut down a summer camp, or relocate a community in advance of a storm.`
   After: `Through that program, it can't force local governments to evacuate a town, shut down a summer camp, or relocate a community in advance of a storm.`
9. Before: `Under current law, the feds don't have zoning authority.`
   After: `Under current law, the feds don't have general local zoning authority.`

Evidence:

- [33 U.S.C. § 1344](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/33/1344), read directly: federal dredged-or-fill-material permit requirements refute the absolute no-construction-authority claim. This is not a finding that Camp Mystic needed or violated a particular permit.
- [44 C.F.R. § 59.24](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/44/59.24), read directly: community floodplain-management standards and probation/suspension demonstrate that FEMA's role is not only communicating risk.
- [44 C.F.R. § 80.11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/44/80.11), read directly: this acquisition program is voluntary, with no eminent domain if negotiations fail. The revised answer is confined to that program instead of asserting that every federal acquisition or emergency power is absent.
- The same [NWS WEA explanation](https://www.weather.gov/mkx/flashflood_updates_WEA), [Commerce OIG report](https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-017-I-SECURED.pdf), and [Texas legislative sequence](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/355/Report-on-the-Camp-Mystic-Flood-Disaster-of-July-4-2025.pdf?d=06182026#page=80) above support the warning-channel and start-time corrections.

Version recommendation: **major**, given materially different legal scope and event chronology. Neighboring repeated zoning and compulsory-relocation absolutes were qualified so they do not contradict the corrected clauses. The institutional-accountability argument and original question structure were preserved.

Remaining hold: unverified local permit/grandfathering and plan-review assertions, specific camp/death-count scope, CodeRED chronology, and causal assignments to county delay not established by a general WEA description. No finding of legal fault, construction illegality or civil liability is asserted by this correction. Existing artwork and captions were not reviewed or cleared here.

## 5. Why the Mexican Navy Ship Cuauhtémoc Crashed Into the Brooklyn Bridge

File: `content/essays/why-the-mexican-navy-ship-cuauhtc3a9moc-crashed-into-the-brooklyn-bridge-c9e21ab4b72e.md`

1. Before: `its sails full`
   After: `its sails furled`
2. Before: `The ship lost power and drifted in reverse into the bridge.`
   After: `Early officials' accounts suggested the ship lost power, but the cause was preliminary. It moved in reverse into the bridge.`
3. Before: `The 2025 voyage spanned 22 ports over 254 days`
   After: `The 2025 voyage was scheduled to span 22 ports over 254 days`
4. Before: `home port in Veracruz`
   After: `home port in Acapulco`

Evidence:

- [NTSB investigation DCA25MM039](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA25MM039.aspx), June 30, 2025 preliminary report, sections 1.1–1.3, read directly: furled sails and collision sequence; investigation remains preliminary. The NTSB record does not establish a completed causal finding or that bridge passage was the planned route.
- [AP's May 18, 2025 account](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2025/05/18/mexican-navy-training-vessel-hits-new-yorks-brooklyn-bridge/), read directly: officials' early power-loss account and expressly preliminary cause. The correction preserves the essay's early-reporting frame rather than rewriting the narrative from later knowledge.
- [Guerrero government, April 6, 2025 voyage announcement](https://www.guerrero.gob.mx/2025/04/zarpa-nuevamente-de-acapulco-el-buque-escuela-cuauhtemoc/), read directly: the 22-port, 254-day itinerary was scheduled, not completed when this May essay was written.
- Home-port evidence is **not inferred from that departure**. [Acapulco municipality's April 6, 2025 account](https://acapulco.gob.mx/2025/04/participa-abelina-lopez-rodriguez-en-ceremonia-de-zarpe-del-buque-escuela-cuauhtemoc/), read directly, describes Acapulco as the vessel's home. [Cozumel municipality's November 7, 2025 account](https://cozumel.gob.mx/prensa/el-buque-escuela-cuauhtemoc-se-despide-de-cozumel-entre-aplausos-y-algarabia/), also read directly, independently identifies its `puerto base en Acapulco, Guerrero`. The public link uses the contemporaneous April account; the later explicit-base record is corroboration. Navy search results also corroborated Acapulco, but inaccessible Navy pages were not treated as inspected evidence.

Version recommendation: **major**, because the operating condition and cause attribution are citation-relevant changes, even though individual home-port and planned-itinerary corrections are narrow.

Remaining hold: unverified political quotations; design-lineage/sail-area claims and annual-tour generalization; and the essay's stronger miscalculation/planning conclusions or implications about a planned bridge passage. AP says the intended departure was away from the bridge; later NTSB operational detail is not permission to reconstruct the essay. No unverified quotation was deleted and no final causal finding was supplied.
