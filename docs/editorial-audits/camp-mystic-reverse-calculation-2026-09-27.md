## 1. Clearance-time arithmetic

One possible reconstruction of the published range is:

| Component | Minutes | Evidence status |
|---|---:|---|
| Mobilization | 20–40 | Existing author assumption; not independently measured |
| Illustrative 500-foot walk | 8–10 | 500 ft at 1 ft/s takes 8 minutes 20 seconds; the upper value is a rounded allowance |
| Combined baseline | 28–50 | Arithmetic, roughly the stated 30–50 inclusive interval |
| Additional disruption allowance | 0–20 | **Back-inferred**, not recovered from a workbook, observation, or cited model |
| Combined scenario | 28–70 | Roughly 30–70 after rounding; not proof of whole-camp clearance |

The prose already mentions panic, weather, debris and stragglers, but does not quantify their delay. Twenty additional minutes makes the upper endpoint fit; that is a possible explanation, not validation of the allowance. Using the full stated 300–700-foot route range gives 5–11.67 minutes of travel and 25–51.67 minutes with mobilization, before queues or other delays.

For groups moving partly in parallel, a general bookkeeping identity is:

`total clearance = max over groups(mobilization + departure wait + travel)`

The waiting term depends on people per group, concurrent groups/routes, escorts, return trips and bottleneck capacity. The article supplies neither those relationships nor a consistent population for this calculation. An estimate for one moving group cannot establish completion time for hundreds of campers. The reconstruction does not substantiate the claim that 100 people cannot move in one wave.

## 2. Where the 32-foot number might come from

The existing elevation profile has a highlighted ground point at **561.1 metres**. Its lower end visually appears around **551–552 metres**. The difference is **9.1–10.1 metres, or 29.86–33.14 feet**. This could explain a rounded 32-foot local-relief figure. It does not identify the original calculation.

Conversely, forcing exactly 32 feet below 561.1 metres gives a base of **551.3464 metres**. This is reverse fitting, not a surveyed river level. The lower endpoint is not established as the contemporaneous water surface, and the highlighted point is not an identified cabin floor or safe-ground threshold.

The map matches the Guadalupe River campus and its Flats, not the separate Cypress Lake campus. Its bent transect is not a whole-camp hydraulic section. [Official legislative maps, pp. 54–55](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/355/Report-on-the-Camp-Mystic-Flood-Disaster-of-July-4-2025.pdf?d=06182026#page=54).

## 3. Why this is not a Hunt-stage conversion

Current USGS metadata gives Hunt's gauge zero as **1,722.82 feet NAVD88**. Under that setting, stages of 15, 25 and 32 feet mean water-surface elevations of **1,737.82, 1,747.82 and 1,754.82 feet at Hunt**, respectively—not at Camp Mystic. Applicability of that present metadata to the July 2025 instrument has not been established. [USGS station](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/tx/nwis/uv/?site_no=08165500).

The camp is upstream on the South Fork; Hunt is downstream of the North/South Fork confluence. A common elevation datum alone does not establish the event-specific water-surface slope, tributary effects or travel time. USGS's mapping guidance specifically uses Hunt as an example of a major confluence limiting upstream map-library extension. [USGS InFRM guidance, p. 8](https://webapps.usgs.gov/infrm/pubs/FDST%20Map%20Submission%20Guidelines%20_vAug19.pdf).

A valid conversion needs, at minimum:

`Hunt threshold = cabin-floor elevation − Hunt gauge-zero elevation − camp-to-Hunt water-surface elevation difference`

All elevations must share a reference system; the final difference requires an independently supported hydraulic/time relationship. Solving for that unknown difference from the desired 15/25/32 outputs would be circular.

The official appendix lists lowest floors including Bug House **1,841.52 feet**, Look Inn **1,843.21**, Twins **1,844.23**, and Rec Hall **1,846.24**. The appendix omits their vertical datum. Those are useful inputs, not a gauge conversion. [Legislative report, pp. 111–112](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/355/Report-on-the-Camp-Mystic-Flood-Disaster-of-July-4-2025.pdf?d=06182026#page=111).

The [2013 FEMA LOMA](https://map1.msc.fema.gov/data/48/L/13-06-4293A-480419.pdf?LOC=9e42a7c443b38710ed0c407071fa661f) identifies NAVD88 but uses building numbers and adjacent grades, not named cabin floors. The [FEMA flood-insurance study, profile 19P](https://map1.msc.fema.gov/data/48/S/PDF/48265CV000B.pdf) provides probability-based water-surface profiles, not a July 4 Hunt-to-camp transfer. Neither supplies the missing conversion.
