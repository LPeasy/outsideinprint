### Declaring Equality - HOLD

1. `By 1979, only 35 states had ratified the amendment, leading Congress to extend the deadline to 1982.` -> `By 1977, 35 states had ratified the amendment; Congress extended the deadline in 1978 to 1982.` [CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R42979.html), ratification/extension history, gives the 1977 count and 1978 votes.
2. `deadlines for amendments are not constitutionally required and have been altered or removed in the past, such as with the 27th Amendment, which took over 200 years to ratify.` -> `deadlines for amendments are not constitutionally required; the 27th Amendment, which took over 200 years to ratify, never had one.` The same CRS report distinguishes the ERA deadline from the deadline-free Twenty-Seventh Amendment.

### Federalism - HOLD

1. `Constitution's ratification in 1787` -> `Constitution's drafting in 1787 and ratification in 1788`. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-q-and-a), ratification answer: ninth state June 21, 1788.
2. `while reserving all others to the states under the Tenth Amendment.` -> `while reserving powers neither delegated to the United States nor prohibited to the states to the states or the people under the Tenth Amendment.` [Amendment text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/tenth_amendment).
3. `NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp. (1937), which rejected strict dual sovereignty.` -> `NLRB v. Jones & Laughlin Steel Corp. (1937), which upheld federal regulation of labor relations closely connected to interstate commerce.` [Opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/301/1), close-and-substantial-relation discussion. It did not abolish dual sovereignty.
4. `Beginning with Ronald Reagan's administration, New Federalism sought` -> `Associated with both Richard Nixon's and Ronald Reagan's administrations, New Federalism sought`. The [Nixon archive](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/finding-aids/st-states-territories-white-house-central-files-subject-files) already identifies Nixon's New Federalism; the [Reagan archive](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/subject/whorm-federal-government-organizations-fg-fg002) records the later program.

### Deference Lost - HOLD

1. `every significant policy decision will face heightened judicial review, replacing administrative discretion with stricter legal scrutiny.` -> `courts must independently review statutory interpretation while respecting discretion Congress has delegated.` [Loper Bright](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/22-451) preserves delegated discretion and distinguishes statutory interpretation from policymaking.
2. `making bureaucratic responsiveness to executive intent dependent on explicit congressional authorization and judicial approval.` -> `requiring independent judicial interpretation of statutes while preserving delegated agency discretion.` Same holding; the original implies universal advance judicial approval.
3. `Why Loper Bright Slowed Down Agency Responsiveness` -> `Why Loper Bright Could Slow Agency Responsiveness`; the two body instances of `further slowing an already complex process` -> `potentially slowing an already complex process`; `the machinery of governance has not just slowed down` -> `the machinery of governance may slow down`. The case establishes a review standard, not measured processing delays. These are evidence qualifications, not a change to the author's causal hypothesis.
4. `challenges to EPA vehicle emissions rules show how agencies may have to defend statutory authority more directly than they did under Chevron` -> `challenges to EPA vehicle emissions rules show how agencies defend statutory authority, although the cited lawsuit preceded Loper Bright`. The existing [RFA announcement](https://ethanolrfa.org/media-and-news/category/news-releases/article/2024/06/rfa-nfu-file-lawsuit-challenging-epa-s-unlawful-vehicle-emissions-standards) dates the filing before the June 28 ruling. The complaint is an allegation, not a finding.
