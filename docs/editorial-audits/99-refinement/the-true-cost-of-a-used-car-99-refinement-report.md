## Scope and Current Assessment

The essay explains a dealer's purchase decision through expected resale proceeds, preparation expenses, time in inventory and uncertainty. It opens with an explicitly imagined buyer's visit, follows the dealership's earlier buying decision, and returns to the same negotiation. The opening and closing are illustrative scenes, not reported testimony or evidence about an actual dealer's thoughts, costs or minimum acceptable price.

The economic reasoning is appropriately bounded. A purchase spread is distinguished from profit. The closing separates costs already incurred from the forward-looking choice between an offer and a potentially costly wait. The text does not claim that past expenses guarantee a particular selling price, that every dealer finances every vehicle, or that all borrowed funds carry one industry-wide rate. The ledger illustration is an estimated buying-limit model, not a valuation guarantee or a measured market result.

The two company filings provide named examples of sourcing and accounting practices. The OCC handbook supplies the inventory-loan interest mechanism. None is presented as a neutral survey of all dealerships. The company's Facebook advertising page identifies its own service and the commercial link's destination; it is not evidence of advertising effectiveness. The contributor's manager/content-writer relationship is disclosed. The author bio remains self-supplied, plain text, without a company-homepage link.
