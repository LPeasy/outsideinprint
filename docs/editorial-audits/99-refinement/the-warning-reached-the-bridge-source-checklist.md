## Analysis Boundary

- The organizational-risk argument, cross-industry examples, ethical duties, and reversibility principle are the author's analysis. No inquiry link is attached to make the later hypothetical management chain look like a documented Titanic personnel sequence.
- The metaphor of a warning failing to cross an organization does not mean no captain or officer received any warning.
- The conclusion does not claim certainty that a particular precaution would have prevented the disaster.

## Reuse as the Archive Reference

1. Identify factual claims and separate them from interpretation before adding links.
2. Read the best available primary passage; record the exact destination, scope, checked date, and any qualification. A working URL alone is not evidence of support.
3. Link a short descriptive phrase close to the supported claim. Reuse verified sources within a piece, but never bulk-link matching keywords across the archive.
4. Flag unsupported claims for qualification or editorial decision; do not attach a plausible-looking citation. Disclose factual clarifications in the edition and revision history.
5. Update the existing per-piece audit, inspect one rendered preview, and use the established publication gate when release is requested. No new citation service, template, or test suite is required.
