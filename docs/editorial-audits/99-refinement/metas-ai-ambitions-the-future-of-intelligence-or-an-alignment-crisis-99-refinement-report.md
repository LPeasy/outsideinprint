## Editorial Philosophy Audit

Decision: PASS

- Evidence: PASS — The corrected financial category, management predictions, technology comparison, privacy disclosure, institutional reference, and research reference are tied to contemporary primary sources. Unsupported assertions of past misuse and universal oversight were removed. This report does not certify the underlying companies' claims independently.
- Logic: PASS — The revised spending argument no longer uses a company-wide capital budget as a proxy for an AI-only or safety-research budget. Model capability is separated from deployment autonomy and software permissions. The control concern is framed as a question, not an outcome proven by company spending.
- Incentives: PASS — The piece identifies leadership, adoption, personalization, engineering efficiency, and downstream misuse as distinct motivations. It does not infer that a commercial incentive alone proves unsafe conduct.
- Tradeoffs: PASS — The essay weighs innovation and access against loss of centralized control and misuse risks, and distinguishes privacy safeguards from alignment work. It acknowledges Meta's community-scrutiny argument without treating it as dispositive.
- Consequences: PASS — Potential effects on information quality, engineering work, downstream operators, and human control are connected to described mechanisms. Self-modification depends on granted access rather than arising automatically from coding skill.
- Uncertainty: PASS — The 2025 frame is explicit; predictions, aspirations, company statements, conditional risks, and verified disclosure contents remain distinct. Lack of a separately reported safety amount is not treated as lack of safety expenditure or proof of inadequate safeguards.
- Institutional Behavior: PASS — Meta, hosted providers, downstream operators, users, and bad actors are distinguished by their control over access, deployment, and resulting risks. Neither open nor closed distribution is presented as inherently safe.
